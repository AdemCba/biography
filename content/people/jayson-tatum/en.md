## From Missouri to Duke

Tatum was born on 3 March 1998 [s:nba-tatum-bio] [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. NBC Sports Boston's 2020 profile lists him at 6 feet 8 inches and 210 pounds [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].

He went to Chaminade College Prep in Creve Coeur, Missouri, where he was rated a five-star recruit and the No. 3 overall prospect in his class [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. As a senior in 2015-16 he averaged 29.6 points and 9.1 rebounds [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. In 2016 he was named Gatorade National Player of the Year and a McDonald's All-American [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. He committed to Duke during his junior year [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].

He spent one season at Duke. He played 29 games and averaged 16.8 points and 7.3 rebounds [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. There he was named to the ACC All-Freshman Team, made third-team All-ACC and was on the first team of the ACC Tournament [s:nba-tatum-bio]. Duke was a No. 2 seed in the 2017 NCAA Tournament and lost to No. 7 South Carolina in the second round [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. He then skipped his sophomore season to enter the draft [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].

## Draft and early seasons with Boston

Boston picked Tatum third overall in the 2017 draft. The team's announcement read: "With the third pick of the 2017 NBA Draft, the Boston Celtics select Jayson Tatum from Duke University" [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. To get him, Boston traded down with the Philadelphia 76ers and passed on Markelle Fultz, the consensus No. 1 pick [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].

As a rookie he averaged 13.9 points, 5.0 rebounds and 1.6 assists, and Boston finished as the East's No. 2 seed [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. In the 2018 playoffs he averaged 18.5 points and 4.4 rebounds across three series. In Game 7 against Cleveland he posterized LeBron James [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. He made the NBA All-Rookie First Team in 2018 and won the Skills Challenge in 2019 [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. In 2018-19 he averaged 15.7 points, 6.0 rebounds and 2.1 assists [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].

He was an NBA All-Star for the first time in 2020 [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio]. Shortly before that game he had scored 39 points in a double-overtime win over the Los Angeles Clippers [s:nbcs-boston-tatum-bio].
