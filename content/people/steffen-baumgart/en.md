## A Rostock-born player who captained Union

Steffen Baumgart was born in Rostock on 5 January 1972 [s:fotmob-profile] [s:bundesliga-union-return]. He played for Hansa Rostock, where a 90min commentary counts nearly 200 appearances [s:90min-union-tribute]. He joined Union Berlin in the summer of 2002 and spent two seasons there, with 68 competitive appearances and 22 goals [s:bundesliga-union-return]. Union named him its player of the year twice, and he captained the side [s:zdfheute-union-return]. From July 2004 to June 2008 he played for Energie Cottbus [s:fotmob-profile].

## Paderborn, then a Cologne job

Before Cologne he had spent more than four years at SC Paderborn [s:sport1-koeln-hire]. On 11 May 2021, 1. FC Köln announced him as the successor to Friedhelm Funkel, on a contract through 2023 that started on 1 July [s:sport1-koeln-hire]. Horst Heldt, then Cologne's managing director, called him an emotional leader who suited the club ("ein emotionaler Leader, der sehr gut zum FC passt") [s:sport1-koeln-hire].

The Cologne spell ended on 21 December 2023. The club announced it the day after a 0:2 defeat at Union Berlin and called it a joint decision. Cologne were 17th with 10 points at the time [s:sport1-koeln-exit].

## Hamburg, then back to Union

Hamburger SV dismissed him on 24 November 2024, one day after a 2:2 draw with Schalke in which HSV had led 2:0 [s:zdfheute-hsv-exit]. The club said it needed "a new impulse" after four league games without a win [s:zdfheute-hsv-exit].

Union announced on 30 December 2024 that he would succeed Bo Svensson, who had been dismissed after Christmas [s:zdfheute-union-return]. It was more than two decades after he had left Union as a player [s:zdfheute-union-return]. Horst Heldt, who had brought him to Cologne in 2021, was by then Union's managing director [s:zdfheute-union-return]. On taking the job he said he was there "to get it going" ("Ich bin hier, um es ans Laufen zu bringen") [s:berliner-zeitung-union-hire].

On 14 January 2026 Union extended his contract early. The club did not give the length of the new deal, and Union were ninth after 16 rounds [s:blick-union-extension]. Baumgart said the club had "created a stable base" to build on and wanted to be successful [s:blick-union-extension].

## The Heidenheim defeat and his dismissal

Union lost 1:3 at Heidenheim, the league's bottom-placed side, on Saturday 11 April 2026 [s:berliner-kurier-union-dismissal]. The club announced his release shortly after midnight [s:berliner-kurier-union-dismissal]. Sporting director Horst Heldt said the performance in Heidenheim was the decisive factor, and that Baumgart was told that evening, before the squad [s:union-berlin-press-dismissal]. Heldt praised his work, said he had taken over the team in a difficult situation, and said Baumgart had been given a chance to say goodbye to the players [s:union-berlin-press-dismissal].

Assistants Danilo de Souza and Kevin McKenna also left [s:union-berlin-press-dismissal]. U19 coach Marie-Louise Eta took charge until the end of the season [s:union-berlin-press-dismissal], and she became the first woman to coach a men's Bundesliga club [s:berliner-kurier-union-dismissal].

## Without a club since April

On 22 May 2026 Berliner Kurier reported that he was back in public view six weeks after his dismissal [s:berliner-kurier-six-weeks]. The same piece said he wanted to get back onto the bench [s:berliner-kurier-six-weeks]. By 14 June, Berliner Zeitung reported that he had gone more than 60 days without a club [s:berliner-zeitung-no-club]. The most recent report we found, dated 23 September 2026, said he was available again and asked where he might go next. It named no club [s:berliner-kurier-free-agent].
