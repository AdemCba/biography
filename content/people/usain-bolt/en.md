The two fastest sprint times ever recorded were run four days apart, in the same stadium, by the same man. In August 2009 Usain Bolt ran 9.58 for 100 metres and 19.19 for 200 metres at the World Championships in Berlin [s:wa-958] [s:yahoo-1919]. When the world's best sprinters gathered in Tokyo for the 2025 World Championships, both marks were still the world records [s:wa-records-2025].

## From cricket to the track

Bolt was born on 21 August 1986 in Sherwood Content, a village in Trelawny parish in Jamaica [s:olympics] [s:britannica]. As a boy he cared more about cricket and football than running. It was his high-school cricket coach who pushed him to take athletics seriously [s:olympics].

The switch paid off quickly. In 2002, at 15, he won the 200 metres at the world junior championships [s:britannica]. Six years later he was an Olympic champion.

## Beijing 2008

Bolt's first 100 m world record came in May 2008 [s:britannica]. At the Beijing Olympics that August he lowered it to 9.69 [s:wa-958]. Days later he won the 200 metres in 19.30, another world record [s:britannica]. Jamaica's 4x100 m relay team, with Bolt in it, also won in world-record time [s:britannica].

That relay gold did not last. In January 2017 the International Olympic Committee disqualified the Jamaican team because teammate Nesta Carter's sample from the 2008 Games had tested positive for the banned stimulant methylhexaneamine [s:itv-2017]. Bolt had to give the medal back. That is why the official count is eight Olympic golds, not nine [s:olympics].

## Berlin 2009: 9.58 and 19.19

A year after Beijing, Bolt took 0.11 seconds off his own 100 m record. On 16 August 2009, in Berlin's Olympic Stadium, he ran 9.58. Tyson Gay finished second in 9.71, a US national record that was still more than a tenth of a second behind [s:wa-958].

Four days later came the 200 m final. Bolt won in 19.19, again 0.11 faster than his Beijing time, and 0.62 seconds clear of the field [s:yahoo-1919]. The stadium clock first showed 19.20 before the result was corrected [s:yahoo-1919].

## The false start in Daegu

Not every final went to plan. At the 2011 World Championships in Daegu, South Korea, Bolt was disqualified from the 100 m final for a false start. A rule introduced shortly before meant one false start was enough to be thrown out [s:aljazeera-2011]. Yohan Blake won in 9.92 [s:aljazeera-2011]. Bolt walked off with his shirt pulled over his face.

He came back to win the 100 m and 200 m at both London 2012 and Rio 2016 [s:britannica], which made him the winner of the Olympic 100 m at three consecutive Games [s:olympics].

## A painful last race

Bolt chose the 2017 World Championships in London for his farewell. It ended badly. On 12 August, on the anchor leg of the 4x100 m relay final, he pulled up with a hamstring injury and went down on the track while Britain took the gold [s:aljazeera-2017]. He left the sport with 14 World Championship medals [s:aljazeera-2017].

The records stayed behind. Every new sprint champion is still measured against two times Bolt ran in one week in Berlin.
