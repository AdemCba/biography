Nigella Lawson joined The Great British Bake Off as its judge in 2026, and the first episode of the series she judged drew about 7.1 million viewers, according to the Oxford Mail [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. Chef and television presenter is how the Oxford Mail describes her [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings], and the sections below cover the Bake Off appointment, the reviews, the ratings report and her books.

## Replacing Prue Leith

Channel 4 and Love Productions announced on 26 January 2026 that Lawson would become the show's new judge. She replaces Prue Leith, who left after nine series [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. Channel 4's release said her "expertise, empathy and humour are the perfect ingredients for the Bake Off tent" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge].

Lawson said it was daunting to follow in the footsteps of Leith and Mary Berry, whom she called "great dames both" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. Ralph Lee, chief executive of Love Productions, called her "one of the most influential and beloved voices in food" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. Channel 4's Ian Katz referred to her chocolate Guinness cake and described Bake Off and Lawson as "two great British icons" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge].

## Reviews of the first series

Reviews of the opening episodes appeared in late September 2026. A round-up published by the Irish News on 23 September quoted several outlets [s:irish-news-review]. Five stars came from The Telegraph, which said Lawson was "supportive in her judging but not afraid to tell the bakers where they can improve" [s:irish-news-review].

Also five stars, the Guardian called her "a much quieter, more soothing presence" that works with Alison Hammond [s:irish-news-review]. Radio Times gave four stars and said she makes "well-informed and helpful comments" on the bakers' work [s:irish-news-review]. Another verdict, from The Independent, is that she adds "a bit of glamour" to the judging panel, but added that "it is hard to tell how tight her baking analysis is going to be" [s:irish-news-review]. Nothing in the round-up amounts to outright criticism of her judging.

## The ratings story

An Oxford Mail report, republished by Yahoo News UK on 27 October 2026, says that about 7.1 million people watched Lawson's first episode as judge, citing BARB [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. Strictly Come Dancing's first live show, according to the same article, drew about 6.2 million viewers, roughly 1.5 million fewer than the previous series opener [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings].

Lawson gets credit in the headline for helping Bake Off beat Strictly for the first time [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. Whether either figure is overnight or consolidated is not stated, and the Strictly number has no measurement source [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. Also, the shows differ in format, a baking contest against a live dance show, so the numbers are best read as reported figures, not a clean comparison.

## Books

Penguin Random House describes her as the author of bestselling books including How to Eat, How to Be a Domestic Goddess, Nigella Bites, Forever Summer and Feast, and says she is a contributor to the New York Times [s:prh-nigella-lawson]. Penguin's UK author page also lists At My Table, Eating, Simply Nigella and Nigellissima [s:penguin-uk]. Neither publisher page gives publication years, so this page does not date individual titles.
