Nigella Lawson was born in London on 6 January 1960 [s:wikidata-nigella]. Her career runs through cookbooks, television and writing for the New York Times [s:prh-nigella-lawson]. In 2026 it brought her to the judging table of The Great British Bake Off. The sections below cover her books and television series, the judging roles and honours on her record, the Bake Off appointment, the first reviews and the ratings report.

## Cookbooks and television series

How To Eat: The Pleasures and Principles of Good Food, published in 1998, was her first cookbook [s:gfw-nigella-profile]. Penguin Random House now lists it among her bestsellers, alongside How to Be a Domestic Goddess, Nigella Bites, Forever Summer and Feast [s:prh-nigella-lawson].

Feast: Food to Celebrate Life came out in 2005 [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio]. Her Food Network series followed. Nigella Feasts debuted on the channel in the fall of 2006, and Nigella Express launched there in the fall of 2007 [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio]. Nigella Bites aired on E! Entertainment Television and on Style, though the Food Network bio gives no year for it [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio].

Nigella Kitchen: Recipes From the Heart of the Home was published by Hyperion in October 2010 [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio]. Forever Summer with Nigella also aired on Style [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio].

## Judging roles and honours

The Guild of Food Writers lists Lawson as a judge of the Booker Prize in 1998 and as chair of the Samuel Johnson Prize for Non-Fiction in 2000 [s:gfw-nigella-profile]. The same profile records Best Food Personality, voted by readers of Observer Food Monthly in 2014, and Television Personality of the Year at the Fortnum & Mason Awards in 2016 [s:gfw-nigella-profile].

The date of her British Book Awards win is disputed. The Guild profile says she was voted Author of the Year at the British Book Awards in 2000 [s:gfw-nigella-profile]. The Food Network bio dates the same title to 2001 [s:foodnetwork-nigella-bio]. Neither page is the award body's own record, so this article gives both years rather than choosing one.

Lawson was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature in 2026 [s:wikidata-nigella]. JayLit lists her in the society's 2026 cohort of 22 new Fellows [s:jaylit-rsl-fellows-2026].

## Replacing Prue Leith

Channel 4 and Love Productions announced on 26 January 2026 that Lawson would become the show's new judge. She takes over from Prue Leith, who left after nine series [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. The release said that "Nigella's expertise, empathy and humour are the perfect ingredients for the Bake Off tent" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge].

In the same release, Lawson spoke of following Leith and Mary Berry, whom she called "great dames both" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. Ralph Lee, chief executive of Love Productions, called her "one of the most influential and beloved voices in food" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge]. Ian Katz of Channel 4 mentioned her chocolate Guinness cake and spoke of "two great British icons" [s:ch4-press-bake-off-judge].

## Reviews of the first series

The first reviews of the opening episodes appeared in late September 2026. A round-up published by the Irish News on 23 September gathered verdicts from several outlets [s:irish-news-review]. The Telegraph gave five stars and said Lawson was "supportive in her judging" [s:irish-news-review].

The Guardian also gave five stars, calling her "a much quieter, more soothing presence" [s:irish-news-review]. Radio Times praised her "well-informed and helpful comments" [s:irish-news-review]. The Independent said she adds "a bit of glamour" to the panel but wondered how tight her baking analysis would be [s:irish-news-review]. The round-up contains no outright criticism of her judging [s:irish-news-review].

## The ratings story

The Oxford Mail article, republished by Yahoo News UK on 27 October 2026, says more than 7.1 million people reportedly watched Lawson's first episode as judge [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. It credits the figures to the Broadcasters' Audience Research Board, and puts the first live show of Strictly Come Dancing at 6.2 million viewers [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings].

The headline says Lawson helped Bake Off beat Strictly Come Dancing in a TV first [s:oxford-mail-bake-off-ratings]. The two shows have different formats, a baking contest and a live dance show, so the numbers are best read as reported figures rather than a clean comparison.

## Books in print

Penguin Random House describes her as the author of bestselling books, including How to Eat, How to Be a Domestic Goddess, Nigella Bites, Forever Summer and Feast, and says she contributes to the New York Times [s:prh-nigella-lawson]. Penguin's UK author page adds At My Table, Eating, Simply Nigella and Nigellissima [s:penguin-uk]. Neither publisher page gives publication years for these titles, which is why the dates above come from the Food Network and Guild of Food Writers pages.
