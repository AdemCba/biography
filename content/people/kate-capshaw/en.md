Kate Capshaw turned 73 on 3 November 2026, and these days she is better known in museums than in multiplexes. She was born on 3 November 1953 in Fort Worth, Texas [s:tmdb] [s:rotten-tomatoes], played Willie Scott in *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom* [s:rotten-tomatoes], and now works as a portrait painter whose subjects range from unhoused teenagers to Steven Spielberg [s:kc-studio-about] [s:artdaily-npg-2025].

## Early life: Fort Worth, Missouri and a classroom

Capshaw was born in Fort Worth and raised in Ferguson, Missouri [s:kc-studio-about]. Her own studio site gives a plain education record: a bachelor's degree in education and a master's degree in special education, both from the University of Missouri-Columbia [s:kc-studio-about]. Rotten Tomatoes adds that she taught children with learning disabilities before moving to New York to act [s:rotten-tomatoes].

The teaching years were not a footnote. According to her studio biography, she set up the first primary program (kindergarten to fifth grade) for children with learning disabilities in rural Southern Boone County, Missouri, and spent one year as an itinerant teacher serving high school students with learning challenges [s:kc-studio-about].

She is known professionally as Kate Capshaw [s:tmdb]. The surname comes from her first marriage, to Robert Capshaw, which ended in divorce in 1980 [s:hello-2026]. They have a daughter, the actress Jessica Capshaw [s:hello-2026]. Yahoo Entertainment reports that after the divorce she trained in acting and singing [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025].

## How she got her break

Capshaw moved to New York and began on the daytime soap *The Edge of Night* [s:tmdb]. Rotten Tomatoes dates her screen debut to 1981 [s:rotten-tomatoes], and the same site lists the film *A Little Sex* under 1981 as well [s:rotten-tomatoes].

The breakthrough came in 1984. She won the female lead of Willie Scott, a nightclub singer who gets pulled into Indiana Jones's adventure, in *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom*, directed by Steven Spielberg [s:tmdb] [s:rotten-tomatoes] [s:screenrant-willie-scott]. Yahoo Entertainment calls the role her breakthrough [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025]. Spielberg has said the film changed his life: "I wouldn't have the life that I'm living right now if I hadn't made Temple of Doom because that's where I met Kate Capshaw" [s:syfy-wire-2026].

## Career timeline

The same year as *Temple of Doom*, she appeared in *Dreamscape*, *Windy City* and *Best Defense* [s:rotten-tomatoes]. Rotten Tomatoes says she had leading roles in *Windy City* and *SpaceCamp* [s:rotten-tomatoes]. Hello Magazine counts ten films in the 1980s [s:hello-2026].

## Marriage to Steven Spielberg and family

Capshaw married Spielberg in October 1991, after converting to Judaism [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025]. Yahoo dates the start of their relationship to 1983, on the set of *Temple of Doom* [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025]. Hello Magazine says they have been married for 35 years [s:hello-2026].

Both had children from earlier relationships. Hello Magazine lists Jessica (with Robert Capshaw) and Max (Spielberg's son with Amy Irving) [s:hello-2026]. Together they have Theo, Sasha, Sawyer, Mikaela and Destry [s:hello-2026]. Yahoo adds that Sasha was born in 1990, that Theo is a child Capshaw had been fostering and who was adopted, and that Sawyer, Mikaela and Destry followed [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025]. Syfy Wire summarises it as three biological children and one adopted [s:syfy-wire-2026]. TMDB says she has seven children in all, counting Jessica [s:tmdb]; the figure fits a blended family, since the five children she shares with Spielberg are joined by Jessica and Max.

Yahoo quotes a 1995 interview in which she said of her husband: "He operates his jet, a camera and a story. My jet is the family." [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025] At a SXSW panel in March, Spielberg said "Kate has made such a life for me" [s:syfy-wire-2026]. In 2022 Spielberg directed Marcus Mumford's "Cannibal" music video, his only one, and Capshaw served as producer and art director [s:yahoo-spielberg-wife-2025].

## A second career as a painter

Capshaw began concentrated art studies in 2009, working in figurative charcoal, watercolor, photography and oil painting, and has focused on portraiture since [s:kc-studio-about]. In 2016 she began *Unaccompanied*, a series that uses portraits to address youth homelessness in the United States [s:kc-studio-about]. Pérez Art Museum Miami says she made it with community youth centers across the country, starting with portraits in Los Angeles and later in San Francisco, Minneapolis, Chicago, St. Louis and New York [s:pamm-exhibition-2024].

In 2019 she was selected as a finalist in the Outwin Boochever Portrait Competition at the Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery, and the touring show *The Outwin 2019: American Portraiture Today* reached Springfield, Massachusetts (2020-21) and the Kemper Art Museum in St. Louis (2022) [s:kc-studio-about]. Her portraits were also shown in 2023 at the Bo Bartlett Center in Columbus State University, at Henry Street Settlement in New York and at The Art Show at the Park Avenue Armory, and in 2024 at the New York Academy of Art [s:kc-studio-about].

Her largest museum project so far was *Exclusive Tonsorial Services* at Pérez Art Museum Miami, from 21 November 2024 to 19 January 2025 [s:pamm-exhibition-2024]. Her studio calls it her first solo museum installation [s:kc-studio-about]. It pays tribute to the Miami barber Sergei "Sirj" Grant, with whom she talked for months while developing it [s:pamm-exhibition-2024]. A public talk with Capshaw, Grant and museum director Franklin Sirmans took place on 3 December 2024 [s:pamm-exhibition-2024].

## Painting Spielberg

In 2025 the National Portrait Gallery commissioned Capshaw to paint her husband, one of four 2025 Portrait of a Nation honorees alongside Jamie Dimon, Temple Grandin and Joy Harjo [s:kc-studio-about] [s:artdaily-npg-2025]. The work, *The Picture Maker*, combines oil painting with film projection [s:kc-studio-about]. She sized the canvas to the 1:33 aspect ratio of 8 mm film, Spielberg's first medium, and the projection includes archival films he made in his teens and early twenties that had never been shown publicly [s:artdaily-npg-2025].

The sources disagree on how long it stays on view. Her studio site says through 17 October 2027 [s:kc-studio-about], while Art Daily says the portraits are on view through 8 November 2026 [s:artdaily-npg-2025]. Check the museum before travelling. Hello Magazine also reports that in 2026 a portrait of Tafari Campbell was included in the Obama Presidential Center [s:hello-2026].
