## From New Zealand karts to Formula 2

Lawson won two New Zealand karting national titles in 2014 [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. In 2016 he won the New Zealand F1600 Championship as its youngest ever champion, with 14 wins [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. In 2017 he moved to Australian Formula 4 with Team BRM and finished runner-up with five wins [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. The following year he raced in ADAC Formula 4 for Van Amersfoort Racing, again as runner-up, with three wins [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. He then joined the Red Bull Junior Team [s:visacashapprb-lawson-profile].

In 2019 he won the New Zealand Toyota Racing Series, finished second in the Euroformula Open and raced in the FIA Formula 3 Championship with MP Motorsport, where he finished 11th with 41 points and two podiums [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. In 2020 he moved to Hitech Grand Prix in Formula 3, won three races and finished fifth with 143 points [s:racingnews365-driver-profile].

Formula 2 followed in 2021 with Hitech. The FIA lists him 11th in the drivers' standings, with one win in the first Sakhir Sprint Race [s:fia-f2-hall-of-fame]. RacingNews365 gives his finish as ninth [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. In 2022 he moved to Carlin, won three sprint races and finished third with 149 points [s:fia-f2-hall-of-fame]. In 2023 he raced in Japan's Super Formula for Team Mugen and finished runner-up [s:honda-driver-profile], with three wins [s:racingnews365-driver-profile].

## Stepping in for Ricciardo

Lawson made his F1 debut at the 2023 Dutch Grand Prix, replacing Daniel Ricciardo, who had broken his hand [s:f1-driver-profile]. He qualified 19th and finished 13th [s:visacashapprb-lawson-profile]. He was back for the Singapore Grand Prix, finishing ninth for his first two F1 points [s:f1-driver-profile] [s:f1-results-2023]. That season he also raced at Monza, Suzuka and Lusail, finishing 11th, 11th and 17th. All five of his 2023 races were for AlphaTauri [s:f1-results-2023].

He returned in 2024 at the United States Grand Prix and scored points at Austin and at São Paulo [s:visacashapprb-lawson-profile] [s:racingnews365-driver-profile].

## Two races at Red Bull

Lawson was announced as Sergio Pérez’s replacement at Red Bull Racing, alongside Max Verstappen [s:f1-driver-profile]. After two Grands Prix, he was moved back to Racing Bulls [s:f1-driver-profile]. The Honda profile says he began 2025 as a Red Bull Racing driver and moved to Racing Bulls before the Japanese Grand Prix, in a swap with Yuki Tsunoda [s:honda-driver-profile]. RacingNews365 places the switch after the Australian and Chinese Grands Prix [s:racingnews365-driver-profile]. RacingNews365 tells it as a sacking from the Red Bull top team, and says he drove for Red Bull again for three races when Isack Hadjar was injured [s:racingnews365-unjust-sacking-2026]. Neither the F1 profile nor RacingNews365 gives a calendar date for the switch, but both place it after two races [s:f1-driver-profile] [s:racingnews365-driver-profile].

The two accounts do not match, and the sources do not settle which one is right. His best result that year was fifth at Azerbaijan [s:racingnews365-driver-profile].

Looking back in December 2025, Lawson said he had been carrying the pressure of that spell into the start of the year: “I think at the start of the year obviously I was having a lot of those thoughts with what was going on” [s:f1-more-relief-2025]. He said the year had also made him stronger: “there are a lot of things that happened this year that have made me a lot stronger” [s:f1-more-relief-2025].
