Taylor Swift is the first artist to win the Grammy for Album of the Year four times [s:etonline-midnights]. Her most recent headline numbers come from 2025. In October that year her album *The Life of a Showgirl* opened with 4.002 million equivalent album units [s:today-showgirl-2025], and in May she announced that she had bought back the master recordings of her first six albums [s:mbw-masters-2025].

## From Wyomissing to Nashville

Swift was born on December 13, 1989, in West Reading, Pennsylvania [s:foxnews-birth]. ABC27 reports that she grew up in Wyomissing before moving to Nashville [s:abc27-birthplace].
## Four Album of the Year wins

At the Grammy Awards on February 4, 2024, Taylor Swift won Album of the Year for *Midnights*. It was her fourth win in that category, the first time any artist had reached four [s:etonline-midnights]. The Recording Academy's page for the 2024 category names the same winner and lists the other nominees: Jon Batiste, boygenius, Miley Cyrus, Lana Del Rey, Janelle Monáe, Olivia Rodrigo and SZA [s:grammy-aoty-2024].

The same night she also won Best Pop Vocal Album, again for *Midnights* [s:grammy-pop-vocal-2024]. The two categories are separate entries, so one album produced two of her wins that evening.

## Time's Person of the Year

TIME named her its 2023 Person of the Year. The choice was announced live on TODAY on December 6, 2023 [s:nbc-time-poy]. Editor-in-chief Sam Jacobs wrote that "Taylor Swift found a way to transcend borders and be a source of light," and that "No one else on the planet today can move so many people so well" [s:nbc-time-poy].

## Buying back her first six albums

On May 30, 2025, Swift told fans in a handwritten letter that she had acquired the master recordings of her first six studio albums: *Taylor Swift*, *Fearless*, *Speak Now*, *Red*, *1989* and *Reputation* [s:mbw-masters-2025]. Those masters had changed hands in 2020, when Shamrock Capital paid Ithaca Holdings $405 million for them [s:mbw-masters-2025].

The price of the 2025 deal was not disclosed. Music Business Worldwide reports that Variety described it as a "nine-figure sum," and that Page Six had suggested a value of $600 million to $1 billion. Those are press reports, not confirmed figures [s:mbw-masters-2025].

Swift had already re-recorded four of the six albums as *Taylor's Version* releases: *Fearless*, *Red*, *Speak Now* and *1989*. *Reputation* and her self-titled debut have not been re-recorded [s:mbw-masters-2025]. In the letter she wrote that she had "meticulously re-recorded and released 4 of my albums, calling them Taylor's Version," and called the deal her "greatest dream come true" [s:mbw-masters-2025]. She said she had long hoped to "purchase my music outright with no strings attached, no partnership, with full autonomy" [s:mbw-masters-2025].

## The Eras Tour in numbers

Pollstar, the touring trade publication, estimated in December 2023 that the Eras Tour had grossed more than $1 billion, the first tour to pass that mark. Pollstar counted 4.3 million tickets sold at an average price of $238.95 [s:intix-pollstar-2023]. Guinness World Records recognised the tour as the highest-grossing music tour, citing Pollstar's figure of $1.04 billion to date. Its article gave the tour as 151 shows, and noted that Elton John's farewell tour had earned $939 million over 328 shows [s:guinness-eras-2023].

The tour began on March 17, 2023, and ended with a final show in Vancouver on December 8, 2024. Pollstar's final report counts 149 shows across 51 stadiums, and estimates a gross of $2.2 billion from 10.055 million tickets sold [s:pollstar-eras-final-2024]. A press report on the tour's end carries the same $2.2 billion estimate [s:kctv5-pollstar-2024]. The show count therefore differs between the 2023 Guinness article and Pollstar's final tally. Every gross and ticket figure here is a Pollstar estimate.

## The Life of a Showgirl's first week

Billboard, citing the data firm Luminate, reported that *The Life of a Showgirl* debuted with 4.002 million equivalent album units in its first US week [s:today-showgirl-2025]. That total counts streaming as well as sales. Pure album sales were over 3.4 million copies [s:mbw-showgirl-2025]. The album is the first in US history to pass 4 million units in a single week [s:thefader-showgirl-2025]. Music Business Worldwide also reports it as a record for the most albums sold in a week in the United States [s:mbw-showgirl-2025].

The reports say it beat the previous record, which had belonged to Adele's *25* [s:today-showgirl-2025]. The sources do not agree on *25*'s figure. TODAY gives 3.482 million units [s:today-showgirl-2025], while Music Business Worldwide, citing Billboard and Luminate, gives 3.378 million for its opening week in November 2015 [s:mbw-showgirl-2025]. Check both before quoting a number for *25*.

The album also gave Swift her 15th No. 1 on the Billboard 200. That makes her the first solo artist to score 15 chart-topping albums [s:thefader-showgirl-2025]. Her previous album, *The Tortured Poets Department*, had reached 2.61 million equivalent album units in the week after its release, according to Luminate, and gave her her 14th No. 1 [s:nbc-ttpd-2024].

Her official website now sells a digital release, *Patient Zero From The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore*, alongside physical editions of the album [s:taylorswift-official].

## Where the fortune comes from

Forbes lists Swift's net worth at $2 billion, as a real-time figure for October 26, 2026 [s:forbes-profile]. Its breakdown, from an update dated June 3, 2026, gives royalties and touring nearly $800 million, her music catalog an estimated $600 million, and real estate about $110 million [s:forbes-profile]. These are Forbes estimates, not reported earnings. Forbes updates its figures over time, so the $2 billion total quoted here is dated to October 26, 2026.
