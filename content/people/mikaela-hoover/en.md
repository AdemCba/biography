Mikaela Hoover is 42 years old. She was born on 12 July 1984 in Colbert, Washington, near Spokane [s:tmdb], and in 2026 she has an unusually full year: she is on screen as Ava in the second season of *Beef* [s:rt-beef-s2], gives the voice and the face capture to Tony Tony Chopper in Netflix's live-action *One Piece* [s:popculture-ava], and is set to return as Cat Grant in HBO Max's *The People v. Gorilla Grodd* [s:thewrap-grodd]. Behind that sits a working career of about twenty years [s:comicbook-interview], most of it spent in small parts that she kept turning into more.

## Early life and a name she thought about changing

TMDB, the film database, records her birth date and place, and lists her as an American actress who also appears under the name Mikaela Mehrizi Hoover [s:tmdb]. Beyond that, the reliable record of her childhood is thin. We found no on-record interview that covers her school years or her family in detail, so this page does not repeat the biographical claims that circulate on fan and aggregator sites.

One small, checkable detail comes from a 2014 interview with Sharp Magazine. She said she once asked her parents whether she should drop "Hoover" and use her middle name instead, and she never did it [s:sharp-2014]. In the same piece she said that early in her career she was offered a horror film role involving nudity and turned it down [s:sharp-2014]. Horror stayed in her life in other ways. In a later Collider interview she named it as a passion [s:collider-brosnahan], and in 2014 she had credits in the web series *Zombie Basement* and the horror film *Hide & Seek* [s:sharp-2014] [s:tmdb].

## The break: two web series with James Gunn

The relationship that shaped her career began with an audition. She met the director James Gunn when she tried out for *Humanzee!*, a mockumentary released in 2008 [s:collider-brosnahan].

Gunn and the producer Peter Safran created *Sparky & Mikaela* for her [s:slashfilm-gunn]. She played a down-on-her-luck high school student who fights crime alongside a talking raccoon named Sparky, played by Joe Fria, [s:slashfilm-gunn]. Safran is now co-chairman and co-CEO of DC Studios [s:collider-brosnahan]. Hoover describes Gunn as something like a big brother and says she feels close to him and the people around him [s:collider-brosnahan].

Gunn kept casting her. She had a part in his feature *Super* (2010) as Holly [s:tmdb], then a small one in *Guardians of the Galaxy* (2014) as Nova Prime's assistant [s:tmdb] [s:slashfilm-gunn]. SlashFilm lists *The Suicide Squad* and *Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3* among the later Gunn films she appeared in [s:slashfilm-gunn].

## Floor the rabbit

Her best-known voice part is Floor, a rabbit in *Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3* [s:slashfilm-gunn]. ComicBook.com calls it a memorable turn, and she voiced the character [s:comicbook-interview].

## Cat Grant in Superman

In *Superman* (2025) she plays Cat Grant, a gossip columnist at the Daily Planet, who works alongside Clark Kent, Lois Lane and Jimmy Olsen [s:comicbook-interview] [s:collider-brosnahan]. Gunn cast her without the usual audition process [s:comicbook-interview].

Hoover has a clear view of the character. She says Cat is far more interested in Clark than in Superman, and that Cat sees in Clark a kind, steady man of a sort she has not had before [s:comicbook-interview]. She told ComicBook.com she would like to see more of Cat and Clark together in later DC films, though the article does not confirm what she has in the next one, *Man of Tomorrow* [s:comicbook-interview]. She also said she kept a journal during filming and that her friendship with Rachel Brosnahan, who plays Lois Lane, has carried on beyond the shoot [s:collider-brosnahan].

## How she became Chopper

The Chopper story is the one she tells most often. In 2025 she was filming *Beef* and, by her own account, wanted to concentrate on it. When the audition for a voice part arrived through her voice-over agent, the materials did not say which show it was for, and she passed [s:popverse-chopper]. Netflix's casting team then asked for her specifically and asked her to read again. After reading the sides she felt connected to the character, and she learned only at a Zoom callback that the job was Tony Tony Chopper [s:popverse-chopper].

The brief she had been given described "a strange little forest creature that had been kind of picked on his whole life" [s:popverse-chopper]. She imagined a slimy, green, one-eyed outcast [s:comicbook-interview]. Her reading is that Chopper sees himself as a monster rather than as cute, and she plays him that way [s:comicbook-interview]. She calls the emotional scenes in episodes seven and eight of season two heartbreaking [s:comicbook-interview]. She also says her 14-year-old dog is her muse for both Chopper and Floor [s:comicbook-interview].

Season two of *One Piece* reached Netflix on 10 March 2026, and PopCulture.com describes her Chopper as voice and performance capture [s:popculture-ava]. TMDB lists the series under 2025 and also credits her voice in *LEGO One Piece* [s:tmdb].
