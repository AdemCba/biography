## Born in Honolulu, raised in Virginia

Graham was born on 16 March 1967 in Honolulu [s:tv-academy]. Her mother, Donna Grant, was a fashion buyer. Her father, Lawrence Graham, is a candy industry lobbyist and a former president of the National Confectioners Association [s:tv-academy]. According to the Television Academy biography, she lived in Japan for a few years while her father worked for the Agency for International Development in Vietnam [s:tv-academy].

Her parents divorced when she was five, and she moved with her father to Alexandria, Virginia [s:tv-academy]. She graduated from Langley High School in 1984 [s:tv-academy]. Two summers of stock theatre at the Barn Theatre in Augusta, Michigan, earned her an Actors' Equity card in 1988 [s:tv-academy].

Her schooling was academic first. She took a bachelor's degree in English literature at Barnard College in 1988, then moved to Texas in 1992 for a Master of Fine Arts in acting at Southern Methodist University [s:tv-academy]. Her publisher's biography lists the same two degrees [s:prh-author]. The reading and writing habit stayed with her, and it matters for the second half of her career.

## The break: from guest spots to Stars Hollow

After Texas she moved to Los Angeles in the mid-1990s and worked her way up through guest parts. The Television Academy lists appearances on *3rd Rock from the Sun*, *Caroline in the City*, *Law and Order* and *Seinfeld*, plus regular roles in short-lived series including *Good Company*, *Townies*, *Conrad Bloom* and *M.Y.O.B.* [s:tv-academy]. Rotten Tomatoes also lists film work in the late 1990s, among it *One True Thing* in 1998 [s:rotten-tomatoes].

The break was *Gilmore Girls*. From 2000 to 2007 she played Lorelai Gilmore, on The WB [s:rotten-tomatoes]. The series ran for seven seasons and 154 episodes [s:tv-academy]. In 2002 she received a Golden Globe nomination for best actress in a television drama for the role [s:golden-globes]. She did not win, and the Golden Globes site lists no other nominations for her [s:golden-globes].

In her 2016 memoir she described the first season as an "adrenaline-fueled dialogue high", and she said she found the role harder in the final seasons, when the show was made without Sherman-Palladino [s:nme-memoir]. That is her own account, as reported by NME.

## Stage, *Parenthood* and the books

In 2009 Graham made her Broadway debut as Miss Adelaide in the revival of *Guys and Dolls* at the Nederlander Theatre, which ran from March to June [s:tv-academy]. She then took the role of Sarah Braverman in the NBC family drama *Parenthood*. Rotten Tomatoes dates the series from 2010 to 2015, while the Television Academy gives 2009 as her start [s:rotten-tomatoes] [s:tv-academy]. The sources differ by a year on the start; both end in 2015.

She also became a writer. Her first novel, *Someday, Someday, Maybe*, came out in 2013 [s:tv-academy]. In 2016 she published *Talking as Fast as I Can*, a memoir of essays about *Gilmore Girls* and her career, which also covers sexism and ageism in Hollywood [s:nme-memoir]. Penguin Random House describes her as a three-time New York Times bestselling author, including *Talking as Fast as I Can* and *Someday, Someday, Maybe*. Its author page also lists a fourth title, *Have I Told You This Already?* [s:prh-author].

In 2016 she was one of the presenters of the 68th Emmy nominations announcement, alongside Anthony Anderson and Bruce Rosenblum [s:tv-academy]. That was a presenting job, not a nomination: the Television Academy shows no Emmy nominations for her [s:tv-academy]. Her publisher also credits her as executive producer and star of the Disney+ series *The Mighty Ducks: Game Changers* [s:prh-author].

## Relationships and family

Graham has kept most of her private life to herself. What she has said publicly concerns Peter Krause, her co-star on *Parenthood*. In a November 2022 interview reported by Good Morning America, she said the two met and began dating while making the show. The relationship stayed largely private until 2021 [s:gma-krause]. Looking back, she said they "almost came into a relationship as people in their 40s without asking any of the questions people in their 40s should be asking" [s:gma-krause].

She also said the end of the relationship "still has its moments" but that she is in a better place now [s:gma-krause]. She helped raise Krause's son, Roman, who is now an adult, and said "we're connected" [s:gma-krause]. The sources we checked do not mention children of her own, and we do not guess.

## Reminders of Him and the Gilmore Girls book

In 2026 Graham plays Grace Landry in *Reminders of Him*, the film adaptation of a Colleen Hoover novel. The cast also includes Maika Monroe, Tyriq Withers, Rudy Pankow, Lainey Wilson and Bradley Whitford [s:peacock-reminders]. It was released in the United States on 13 March 2026 and is available to stream on Peacock [s:peacock-reminders]. In March 2026 she gave an interview to Extra about it [s:extra-book].

The larger story is the book. On 3 December 2025 *Today* announced an untitled behind-the-scenes *Gilmore Girls* book by Graham and Sherman-Palladino, slated for fall 2027 [s:tvline-book]. Graham confirmed it on Instagram: "Because we love you, Amy and I are working on a new 'Gilmore Girls' book. Get your coffee ready!" [s:tvline-book]. The official logline, obtained by People, calls it "an intimate, behind-the-scenes portrait of their creative partnership" [s:tvline-book]. In March 2026 she told Extra the book will be a story about *Gilmore Girls* and, to her, "a story of friendship" [s:extra-book]. That interview gave no release date [s:extra-book].

On 23 September 2026 Graham attended Variety's Power of Women event at the Beverly Hills Hotel to support her *Gilmore Girls* co-star Melissa McCarthy, who was honoured. There she said she had filmed part of the HBO Max *Gilmore Girls* documentary and found it emotional [s:power-of-women-2026]. We rely on that September 2026 account for the documentary.
