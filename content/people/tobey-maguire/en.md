Tobey Maguire is 51 years old as of 3 November 2026. He was born on 27 June 1975 in Santa Monica, California [s:biography-com] [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. The question that follows him now is not about his age but about his future: Marvel did not list him in the cast it announced for *Avengers: Doomsday* on 26 March 2025, and we found no official confirmation of a return since [s:slashfilm-doomsday-2025].

## A childhood spent moving

Tobias Vincent Maguire was born to Vincent and Wendy, who were young and not married when he arrived. They married soon afterwards, but the marriage did not last [s:biography-com]. He spent much of his early childhood moving between households in California, Washington and Oregon [s:biography-com].

His mother worked as a secretary. She encouraged him to take acting lessons and drove him to auditions in Hollywood [s:biography-com]. By ninth grade he had left school to concentrate on acting [s:biography-com]. Those are the public facts about his family life as a child. We leave out the rest, because the sources we could open do not state it.

## Small parts and a cancelled sitcom

The first screen work was modest. Biography.com lists one- and two-line roles on *Roseanne* and *Blossom* [s:biography-com]. In 1992 he led the series *Great Scott!*, which was cancelled after nine weeks [s:biography-com]. In 1995 he took a small part in *Empire Records*, but he left the production and his scenes were cut [s:biography-com]. He also appeared in *Duke of Groove*, a short film that was nominated for an Oscar [s:biography-com].

None of this made him known to a wide audience. What changed the direction was a run of independent and literary films in the late 1990s. In 1997 he appeared in Woody Allen's *Deconstructing Harry* and played Paul Hood in Ang Lee's *The Ice Storm*, a part that earned critical praise [s:biography-com]. In 1998 he led *Pleasantville*, playing David Wagner [s:biography-com] [s:rt-pleasantville], and had a small role in *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas* [s:biography-com].

## The break: The Cider House Rules and Wonder Boys

The film that gave him a lead in a major studio drama was *The Cider House Rules* (1999), in which he played Homer Wells, an orphan at the centre of the story, alongside Charlize Theron, Michael Caine and Paul Rudd [s:rt-cider-house-rules]. The film received seven Academy Award nominations [s:biography-com]. A year later he played the student writer James Leer opposite Michael Douglas in *Wonder Boys* (2000) [s:biography-com] [s:rt-wonder-boys].

By the end of 2000 he had a record of serious roles in respected films, and then came the biggest part of his career.

## Spider-Man, 2002 to 2007

In *Spider-Man* (2002) Maguire played Peter Parker and the hero of the title [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. Biography.com reports that he was paid $4 million for the role and that the film earned $114 million in its opening weekend, a box-office record at the time [s:biography-com]. Both figures are as reported by that outlet, and we have not checked them against studio filings.

He returned for *Spider-Man 2* (2004) and *Spider-Man 3* (2007) [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. In an interview with CBS News and the Associated Press published on 9 April 2007, a few weeks before the third film opened on 4 May, he said he was unsure whether he would play the character a fourth time but that he "might do another 'Spider-Man' movie" [s:cbs-2007]. He described the character in the third film as becoming arrogant and self-involved after the public adores him [s:cbs-2007].

## Between the films

The Spider-Man years did not stop him from taking other work. In *Seabiscuit* (2003) he played the jockey Johnny "Red" Pollard and was also an executive producer [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. He appeared in *The Good German* (2006) as Tully [s:rt-tobey-maguire], and in a cameo in *Tropic Thunder* (2008) [s:biography-com].

In *Brothers* (2009) he played Sam Cahill [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. The performance brought his only Golden Globe nomination, for best actor in a motion picture drama, listed under 2010 by the Golden Globe Awards [s:golden-globes]. He has no wins listed there [s:golden-globes].

In *The Great Gatsby* (2013) he played Nick Carraway opposite Leonardo DiCaprio [s:biography-com] [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. In *Pawn Sacrifice* he played the chess champion Bobby Fischer and was also a producer [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. Biography.com dates that film to 2015, while Rotten Tomatoes and Entertainment Tonight tie it to 2014, so we use 2014 [s:biography-com] [s:et-babylon-2021].

## Seven years away and a return

After *Pawn Sacrifice* he did not appear in a feature film for years. When *Babylon* was photographed on set in Los Angeles in August 2021, Entertainment Tonight described it as his first on-screen film role since 2014 [s:et-babylon-2021]. In April 2022 The Daily Beast noted that apart from a cameo in *Spider-Man: No Way Home* and a voice part in *The Boss Baby*, he had not starred in a film since 2014 [s:daily-beast-babylon-2022].

*Spider-Man: No Way Home* was released on 17 December 2021 [s:rt-no-way-home]. In *Babylon*, Damien Chazelle's film about Hollywood in the late 1920s, he played James McKay and was an executive producer [s:rt-tobey-maguire]. Casting reports in April 2022 had said he would play Charlie Chaplin [s:daily-beast-babylon-2022]. The later credit lists the role as James McKay, so the early reports and the final credit differ.

## Sobriety and personal life

Maguire has spoken about this himself. In the 2007 CBS interview he said he began attending Alcoholics Anonymous at 19 and described himself as having "an addictive nature" [s:cbs-2007]. Biography.com says he is a vegan and has been sober since 19 [s:biography-com].

He met the jewellery designer Jennifer Meyer in 2003, while he was filming *Seabiscuit* [s:aap-divorce-2026]. They separated in October 2016, and Meyer filed for divorce in October 2020 [s:aap-divorce-2026].
