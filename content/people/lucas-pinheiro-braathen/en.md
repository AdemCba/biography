Lucas Pinheiro Braathen won the men's giant slalom at the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, a gold that made him Brazil's first Winter Olympic champion [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. He raced for Norway until his retirement in 2023 and has raced for Brazil since 2024.

## Born in Oslo, raised between two countries

Braathen was born on 19 April 2000 in Oslo [s:olympiatoppen-athlete]. His father is Norwegian and his mother is Brazilian. He grew up between Norway and São Paulo and is bilingual [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. His listed club is Bærums Skiklub [s:olympiatoppen-athlete].

## Five World Cup wins for Norway

He competed for Norway until 2023. In that time he won five World Cup races and held the world's number one ranking in slalom [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. In the season before he retired he won the slalom Crystal Globe [s:fis-comeback-2024].

## Retirement and the return for Brazil

In October 2023, aged 23, he surprised the sport by retiring [s:fis-comeback-2024]. His account is that he had a falling-out with the Norwegian federation over sponsorship rights [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. He later said that leaving the Norwegian team "sure was difficult" [s:fis-comeback-2024]. Looking back, he said it was hard not "waking up every day in the pursuit of becoming the best in the world at something" [s:fis-comeback-2024].

He bought a one-way ticket to Brazil and settled on Ilhabela, an island off the coast of São Paulo [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. In March 2024 he announced at a press conference in Austria that he would race again, this time for Brazil [s:fis-comeback-2024]. He called Brazil "the country where I discovered my love for sports" [s:fis-comeback-2024]. Under FIS rule 203.5, his application to change his FIS licence registration was due to go to the FIS Council at its spring meeting [s:fis-comeback-2024]. Sources describe him as Norwegian-Brazilian, having competed for Norway until 2023 and for Brazil from 2024 [s:storyproductions-profile-2026].

When he returned to skiing in 2024 he raced in green and yellow, with "Vamos Dançar" written on the back of his helmet [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. Ski Racing's preview of the 2025 season opener at Sölden records that he made his first World Cup start under the Brazilian flag there [s:skiracing-solden-2025-preview]. The 2025 season opener at Sölden, a giant slalom, was scheduled for Sunday, 26 October 2025 [s:skiracing-solden-2025-preview].

## Coming back after a break

After retiring, he went back to Norway to spend time with family, then flew to Brazil [s:fis-comeback-2024]. He contacted the Brazilian federation after former partners said they would support a comeback [s:fis-comeback-2024]. When he announced his return, he said he was "beyond proud" to represent Brazil at World Cups, World Championships and Olympic Games [s:fis-comeback-2024]. He also hoped Norwegian fans would back him "for the person I am, not just based on the flag I represent" [s:fis-comeback-2024].

## Podiums under the Brazilian flag

On 21 December 2025 at Alta Badia, Italy, he finished second in a giant slalom, 0.18 seconds behind Marco Schwarz of Austria [s:dgabc-alta-badia-2025]. That result was his seventh podium for Brazil, and his third in giant slalom, a discipline the article describes as not his specialty [s:infomoney-podiums-2025]. Earlier that season his slalom medals for Brazil were gold at Levi, Finland, silver at Adelboden, Switzerland, and bronze at Kitzbühel, Austria, and Hafjell, Norway [s:dgabc-alta-badia-2025].

## Bormio, 14 February 2026

The men's giant slalom at the Olympics was held on 14 February 2026 in Bormio, Italy [s:storyproductions-profile-2026] [s:nbc-olympics-gold-2026]. His first run was nearly a second faster than that of Switzerland's Marco Odermatt [s:storyproductions-profile-2026]. Olympedia's list of starters includes him representing Brazil [s:olympedia-gs-2026-entries]. His first run was 1:13.92 and his second 1:11.08, for a combined time of 2:25.00 [s:nbc-sports-gold-times-2026]. Odermatt took silver, 0.58 seconds behind [s:storyproductions-profile-2026].

## What the medal meant for Brazil

The gold was the first medal at the Winter Games for any South American country, and it was gold [s:nbc-olympics-gold-2026]. Braathen was also the first South American athlete to reach an Olympic podium in alpine skiing [s:nbc-olympics-gold-2026]. The victory came on the first full day of Carnival, and Brazilian fans gathered at Brazil House in Milan [s:nbc-olympics-gold-2026].

Asked about his background, he put it this way: "Norway taught me how to be an athlete, how to brave the cold. Brazil taught me how to be myself" [s:storyproductions-profile-2026].
