Patrick J. Adams was born on 27 August 1981 in Toronto, Ontario [s:wikidata-q644565] [s:wikidata-q172]. He is best known for playing Mike Ross, the lead in the USA legal drama Suits [s:usc-sda-patrick-j-adams] [s:tvseriesfinale-suits]. His profile on the USC School of Dramatic Arts site also lists film work such as Old School and Pillow Talk [s:usc-sda-patrick-j-adams].

## Seven seasons as Mike Ross

Suits ran on USA from 23 June 2011 to 25 September 2019, for nine seasons and 134 episodes [s:tvseriesfinale-suits]. The role brought Adams a Screen Actors Guild Award nomination for outstanding actor in a drama series [s:usc-sda-patrick-j-adams].

## Why he left after season seven

Adams left the series after its seventh season [s:tvline-suits-exit]. In a 2018 Hollywood Reporter interview, as quoted by Just Jared in 2024, he said the show had told Mike's story: "we've told his story and if he hangs out longer, Mike is just going to be another lawyer on television" [s:justjared-suits-exit-2024]. He had begun to think about leaving at the end of the first half of season six [s:justjared-suits-exit-2024].

In a later conversation on Jesse Tyler Ferguson's podcast Dinner's On Me, he was more direct about his health. He said he had not been taking good care of his mental health and had been drinking too much by the end of season seven [s:tvline-suits-exit]. He also said the only reason to stay was money, and that he never regretted leaving: "It was the right thing for my marriage... It was time" [s:tvline-suits-exit]. His wife is the actress Troian Bellisario [s:justjared-suits-exit-2024].

## Glenn in The Right Stuff

After Suits, Adams was cast as Major John Glenn in The Right Stuff, the Disney+ series about the Mercury Seven astronauts [s:d23-right-stuff-glenn] [s:tvline-right-stuff-preview]. He prepared at length. He said he had "the luxury of an enormous amount of resources", and he spent a couple of days in the John Glenn archives at Ohio State University, which he described as "hundreds and hundreds of boxes of stuff" [s:d23-right-stuff-glenn]. He read Glenn's private letters, journal entries and correspondence with presidents, then kept the material that resonated with him as his "linchpins" instead of doing an impression of the man [s:d23-right-stuff-glenn].

He recalled being told he was the first person cast: "I think I was the first person cast" [s:tvline-right-stuff-preview]. He admitted that the part frightened him, "I had a lot of fear, to be honest", and he saw in Glenn a man who was "the odd man out of the Mercury Seven, and not because he was the best" [s:tvline-right-stuff-preview].

He also described the stretch before the offer. "I was looking to go to sleep for about 365 days," he said, and when the project came along he "just went, 'This is what I've been waiting for'" [s:tvline-right-stuff-preview].

## Broadway debut in Take Me Out

Adams made his Broadway debut as Kippy Sunderstrom in the 2022 revival of Take Me Out [s:justjared-take-me-out-2024]. The production won the 2022 Tony Award for Best Revival of a Play, announced on 12 June 2022, and the play closed the day before at the Hayes Theatre [s:broadwayworld-take-me-out-tony]. The cast list in the same report includes Adams [s:broadwayworld-take-me-out-tony].

The play has nude scenes, and Just Jared reported that Adams had to be naked on stage every night [s:justjared-take-me-out-2024]. "It was so intense that I think I actually truly had to leave my body every night," he added [s:justjared-take-me-out-2024]. He also recalled that director Scott Ellis encouraged him to take the part despite stage panic attacks, telling him it was "your chance to fix that, essentially" [s:justjared-take-me-out-2024].

## The Madison and a false death report

In The Madison, a Paramount+ drama with Michelle Pfeiffer as Stacy Clyburn and Kurt Russell as Preston Clyburn, Adams plays Russell McIntosh [s:paramount-madison-cast]. Russell is the husband of Paige McIntosh, played by Elle Chapman, who is the younger daughter of the Clyburns [s:paramount-madison-cast].

On 29 October 2026 a Facebook page titled "R.I.P. Patrick J. Adams" claimed that he had died "At about 11 a.m. ET on Thursday (October 29, 2026)". The page gained nearly one million likes, and skeptics noted that no major Canadian network had reported the death [s:mediamass-death-hoax]. On 30 October his representatives said he is "still alive and well, stop believing what you see on the Internet" [s:mediamass-death-hoax]. An update dated 31 October 2026 says the story "seems to be false" [s:mediamass-death-hoax].
