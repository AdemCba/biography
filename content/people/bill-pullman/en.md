Bill Pullman is 82 years old as of 3 November 2026. He was born on 17 December 1953 in Hornell, New York, and turns 83 in December [s:tmdb-pullman]. Most viewers know him as President Thomas Whitmore in *Independence Day*, but his résumé runs from a 1987 space parody to a West End play, a Netflix series in 2026 and a sequel due in 2027.

## Early life and the road to acting

Pullman was born in Hornell, New York [s:tmdb-pullman]. A 2017 profile notes that as a boy he worked on farms in upstate New York [s:bww-warren-wilson]. That detail explains something about the adult: he now runs a ranch in Montana and, with his wife Tamara, co-founded a community orchard in Los Angeles [s:bww-warren-wilson].

His first career was in the classroom, not in front of a camera. A 1995 radio listing says he taught drama at the University of Montana and became a department head by the age of 27 [s:ksut-1995]. Montana State University, which gave him an honorary doctorate, describes him as a Hollywood actor "who started his career" at that school [s:msu-bobcat-bulletin]. The two sources name different Montana universities, so this page only says that his early working life was spent teaching in Montana. We do not have a primary source for his degrees, and we leave them out.

## The break: from Ruthless People to Spaceballs

The same 1995 listing says Pullman made his acting debut in *Ruthless People* after the teaching years [s:ksut-1995]. The role that made him recognisable followed soon after. In *Spaceballs* (1987) he played Lone Starr, a Han Solo-like hero, in the space comedy [s:tmdb-pullman] [s:deseret-spaceballs]. In a later interview he said the space scenes in *Spaceballs* and *Independence Day* now look clichéd [s:collider-pullman].

Through the early 1990s he built a reputation as the likeable second lead. He was in *Newsies*, which he also discussed in an interview [s:collider-pullman]. By 1995 he had appeared in *Casper*, *While You Were Sleeping* and *The Last Seduction*, three very different films in one year [s:ksut-1995]. In *Casper* he played Dr. Harvey [s:tmdb-pullman].

## Independence Day and the 1990s

In 1996 Pullman played President Thomas J. Whitmore in *Independence Day*, next to Will Smith, Jeff Goldblum and Judd Hirsch [s:tmdb-pullman] [s:cbs-pullman-2020]. He has called the film's success a surprise: "It was really a surprise and it was a thing that meant a lot to people" [s:cbs-pullman-2020]. He also pointed out why the part was unusual: "at that time there had never really been a heroic lead character that was a President of The United States" [s:cbs-pullman-2020]. On the film's appeal he was dry about it: "Fortunately, people like seeing the White House blown up" [s:cbs-pullman-2020].

The part did not trap him. In 1997 he played Fred Madison in David Lynch's *Lost Highway* [s:tmdb-pullman]. In a 1997 Radio New Zealand interview he described how much freedom Lynch gave actors with their scripts, and compared the film's subplots to *Oedipus Rex* [s:ngataonga-lost-highway]. The same interview mentions his work with the German director Wim Wenders on *The End of Violence*, made after *Lost Highway* [s:ngataonga-lost-highway]. In 1999 he starred as Jack Wells in *Lake Placid* [s:tmdb-pullman].

## Television and the stage

Pullman returned to the presidency on television in *1600 Penn*, an NBC comedy, where he was a series regular [s:tvline-sinner]. He picked up the role again in 2016, reprising Whitmore in *Independence Day: Resurgence*, about twenty years after the original [s:netflix-boroughs] [s:tmdb-pullman].

The biggest television part of his later career was Detective Harry Ambrose in *The Sinner*, which ran from 2017 to 2021 [s:tmdb-pullman]. TVLine reported his casting as a series regular opposite Jessica Biel's character Cora Tanner, a housewife who commits a violent crime without knowing why [s:tvline-sinner]. In a 2020 interview, when the show was in its third season, he said he had never before led a television series [s:cbs-pullman-2020]. He added: "It's unbelievable at my stage of the game that I get to work with so many good actors" [s:cbs-pullman-2020]. In 2014 he also appeared in *The Equalizer* as Brian Plummer [s:tmdb-pullman].

Theatre has stayed part of the mix. In summer 2022 he played Daniel, a tyrannical father dying of emphysema, in the world premiere of Theresa Rebeck's *Mad House* at the Ambassadors Theatre in London, opposite David Harbour, directed by Moritz von Stuelpnagel [s:whatsonstage-mad-house] [s:officialtheatre-mad-house]. The run lasted until 4 September 2022 [s:whatsonstage-mad-house].

## Awards and honours

The USA Network release for the fourth-season renewal of *The Sinner* lists a Screen Actors Guild nomination for Pullman, in the category Male Actor in a Miniseries or TV Movie, for the show's second season [s:spoilertv-sinner-sag]. The same release lists a Critics' Choice nomination for his co-star Carrie Coon, not for him [s:spoilertv-sinner-sag]. We found no major award win in the sources we checked.

Outside the industry, Montana State University announced that it would give him an honorary doctorate at its spring commencement on 5 May 2018 [s:msu-bobcat-bulletin].

## Family and public life

Pullman is married to Tamara, and the couple have two sons, Jack and Lewis, who both attended Warren Wilson College [s:bww-warren-wilson]. Jack earned an art degree, and Lewis earned a social work degree and performed in college theatre [s:bww-warren-wilson]. Lewis is also an actor and appeared in one of his father's films [s:bww-warren-wilson]. In 2011 Bill and Tamara founded Hollywood Orchard, a nonprofit community orchard in Beachwood Canyon in Los Angeles [s:bww-warren-wilson]. In 2017 he gave the commencement address at Warren Wilson College, whose president called him "a deep thinker and an agriculturist" [s:bww-warren-wilson].

## What he is doing now

Pullman's recent work is busy. In 2023 he played Alex Murdaugh in *Murdaugh Murders: The Movie*, which Lifetime called its 500th original movie and aired on 14 and 15 October [s:today-murdaugh]. In 2026 he plays Jack, one of six lead roles in the Netflix supernatural series *The Boroughs*, which the platform's metadata lists as added on 21 May 2026 [s:netflix-boroughs].

On stage, he joins the Royal Shakespeare Company as Gaev in Chekhov's *The Cherry Orchard* at the Swan Theatre in Stratford-upon-Avon, from 10 July to 29 August 2026, alongside Kenneth Branagh and Helen Hunt [s:rsc-cherry-orchard].
