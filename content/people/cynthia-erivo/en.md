Cynthia Erivo is {age} years old. She was born on 8 January 1987 in Stockwell, South London, and has won a Tony, two Grammys and a Daytime Emmy [s:adorocinema-bio] [s:thewrap-oscars] [s:bww-grammy-2026]. She has also been nominated for two Golden Globes for playing Elphaba in the *Wicked* films [s:pinknews-globes]. She has not won an Oscar: at the ceremony on 2 March 2025, where she was nominated for *Wicked*, Mikey Madison won Best Actress for *Anora* [s:oscars-2025].

## Growing up in Stockwell

Erivo was born in Stockwell to Nigerian parents of Igbo heritage, who had lived through the Nigerian civil war [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. She was raised by her mother, Edith, a nurse and single parent, who has spoken publicly about struggling to find food, safety and shelter [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. Erivo later named her production company Edith's Daughter, founded in August 2020 [s:tony-awards-host].

She attended La Retraite Roman Catholic Girls' School on Atkins Road in Clapham Park [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. After school she studied at the University of East London, then moved to the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art (RADA) [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. One account says she briefly studied music psychology before winning her place at RADA [s:ltd-career]. She graduated in 2010 [s:ltd-career].

## First stage work, 2010 to 2014

Her first stage appearance was at the Brighton Festival in 2010 [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. London Theatre Direct lists several early credits from the years that followed, though it does not date each one: *Marine Parade* at the Brighton Festival, *I Was Looking at the Ceiling and Then I Saw the Sky* at Theatre Royal Stratford East, and the Old Vic's 24 Hour Musicals gala [s:ltd-career]. She also toured the UK as Deloris Van Cartier in *Sister Act*, played Chenice in *I Can't Sing!* at the London Palladium, and appeared in the European premiere of *Dessa Rose* at Trafalgar Studios [s:ltd-career].

## The Color Purple

In 2013 Erivo played Celie in *The Color Purple* at the Menier Chocolate Factory in London [s:ltd-career]. In 2015 she repeated the part in the Broadway revival, alongside Jennifer Hudson and Danielle Brooks [s:ltd-career]. The show made her name in New York. She won the Tony Award for Best Actress in a Musical for it [s:thewrap-oscars] [s:ltd-career].

The album followed. On 12 February 2017 the cast recording won the Grammy for Best Musical Theater Album, beating recordings from *Bright Star*, *Fiddler on the Roof*, *Kinky Boots* and *Waitress* [s:playbill-grammy-2017]. Erivo, Brooks and Hudson were the principal soloists and shared the award with the producers and writers [s:playbill-grammy-2017]. At the same ceremony she sang "God Only Knows" with John Legend during the In Memoriam segment [s:playbill-grammy-2017]. The cast also won a Daytime Emmy for performing on the *Today* show [s:thewrap-oscars] [s:ltd-career]. Sources disagree on the year of that Emmy, so this page gives none.

## Screen career

Her first television role was in Michaela Coel's *Chewing Gum* in 2015 [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. The film that changed her screen career was *Harriet* (2019), in which she played the abolitionist Harriet Tubman [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. The nominations announced in January 2020 gave her two Oscar nominations in one year, for Best Actress and, as co-writer of "Stand Up", for Best Original Song [s:thewrap-oscars]. She also received a Golden Globe nomination for the film [s:tony-awards-host].

HBO's *The Outsider* premiered on 12 January 2020 with Erivo in the cast [s:tony-awards-host]. In March 2021 she played Aretha Franklin in National Geographic's *Genius: Aretha*, which brought a 2021 Emmy nomination for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie [s:official-site] [s:tv-academy]. She starred in and produced *Drift*, which premiered at Sundance on 22 January 2023 and reached cinemas on 26 March 2024, and she wrote and recorded its original song "It Would Be" [s:official-site]. In 2023 she appeared opposite Idris Elba in *Luther: The Fallen Sun*, released by Netflix on 10 March [s:brixton-buzz-mbe] [s:tony-awards-host].

## Wicked and Defying Gravity

Erivo played Elphaba, opposite Ariana Grande's Glinda, in Jon M. Chu's film adaptation. Part One was released on 22 November 2024 and Part Two, *Wicked: For Good*, on 21 November 2025 [s:official-site]. Her official site says the first film opened at number one and became the highest-grossing film based on a Broadway musical at the domestic box office [s:official-site]. Brixton Buzz reports that it grossed more than $750 million worldwide [s:brixton-buzz-mbe].

For the first film she was nominated for a Golden Globe, a SAG Award, a Critics' Choice Award, an NAACP Award, a BAFTA and an Academy Award, according to her site [s:official-site]. For the second she received a 2026 Golden Globe nomination for Best Actress in a Comedy or Musical, which made her the first Black woman nominated twice in that category [s:pinknews-globes]. The Academy nominations announced on 22 January 2026 included nothing for *Wicked: For Good*, and Erivo and Grande were not among the nominees [s:pinknews-oscars-2026].

Her second Grammy came in early February 2026. She and Grande won Best Pop Duo/Group Performance for "Defying Gravity", beating HUNTR/X, Katseye, Rosé, Bruno Mars and SZA with Kendrick Lamar [s:bww-grammy-2026]. BroadwayWorld counts two wins from five nominations [s:bww-grammy-2026].

## Hosting, music and writing

Erivo hosted the 78th Annual Tony Awards at Radio City Music Hall, shown live on CBS on 8 June 2025 [s:tony-awards-host]. The Television Academy lists two further 2026 Emmy nominations tied to her: as producer and host of that Tonys ceremony, and as host of *Wicked: One Wonderful Night* on NBC [s:tv-academy]. It also lists a 2025 nomination for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Comedy Series for *Poker Face* [s:tv-academy].

Her debut album *Ch. 1 Vs. 1* came out on 17 September 2021, and her children's book *Remember to Dream, Ebere* on 28 September 2021 [s:tony-awards-host]. Her site also lists *Christmas With Cynthia Erivo* and the *Wicked* soundtracks among her releases [s:official-site].

## What she is doing now

In 2024 Erivo became the first Vice President of RADA, an honorary role [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. In November 2025 she went back to La Retraite, where she sang with pupils and answered questions about her career [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. In August 2025 she played Jesus opposite Adam Lambert's Judas in a concert staging of *Jesus Christ Superstar* at the Hollywood Bowl [s:ltd-career].

In 2026 she starred in Kip Williams's *Dracula* at the Noël Coward Theatre in London, playing all 23 roles, with a run scheduled until 30 May [s:timeout-dracula] [s:brixton-buzz-mbe]. When the Tony Awards announced her as host in February 2025, they also named three projects still to come: the Blue Fairy in Disney's live-action *Pinocchio* directed by Robert Zemeckis, Apple's anthology series *ROAR*, and a biopic of Sara Forbes Bonetta that she will produce and star in [s:tony-awards-host].
