Paulo Fonseca is the new head coach of Sporting CP. A Bola reports that Sporting SAD confirmed the appointment on 4 November 2026, and Lyon announced the same day that he was leaving [s:abola-sporting-official] [s:afp-lyon-departure]. He replaces [Rui Borges](person:rui-borges) [s:abola-sporting-official].

## November 2026: from Lyon to Sporting

A Bola says Sporting SAD confirmed the move in a statement to the CMVM, Portugal's securities regulator, on Wednesday 4 November [s:abola-sporting-official]. His contract runs until June 2030, and five assistants signed deals to 30 June 2030 [s:abola-sporting-official]. A Bola quotes Fonseca saying he would fulfil a childhood dream [s:abola-sporting-official]. The newspaper also says Sporting paid Lyon 5 million euros, and Lyon's own statement, as reported by RTP, says the same sum is due for the early end of his contract [s:abola-sporting-official] [s:rtp-lyon-confirms]. His Lyon deal had been due to run to 30 June 2027 [s:afp-lyon-departure].

Lyon took the news badly. According to RTP, the club called it an unexpected decision and said that, given his clearly stated wish, the board did not think it was in the club's interest to keep working together [s:rtp-lyon-confirms]. AFP quotes Lyon's sporting director, Matthieu Louis-Jean, calling it "un choix du coeur concernant son club d'enfance" [s:afp-lyon-departure]. Lyon were third in Ligue 1 after nine matchdays [s:afp-lyon-departure]. His assistant Jorge Maciel, 41, takes the team for the Europa League tie with Real Sociedad on Thursday and the league trip to Brest on Sunday, and Louis-Jean said Carlos Vicens is among the candidates for the permanent job [s:afp-lyon-departure].

At Sporting, the departure of Borges and his staff was confirmed alongside the hiring [s:abola-sporting-official]. RTP says Borges was removed on Sunday after two years in charge, having won a league and cup double that Sporting had not managed in 23 years [s:rtp-lyon-confirms]. A Bola's first report of the deal, on 1 November, turned out to be accurate [s:abola-sporting-report] [s:abola-sporting-official].

## From Nampula to Barreirense

Fonseca was born on 5 March 1973 in Nampula, Mozambique [s:acmilan-appointment] [s:asroma-10-things]. Mozambique was then a Portuguese colony and gained independence in 1975 [s:asroma-10-things]. He lived in Africa until he was 14, then his family moved to Barreiro, near Lisbon [s:asroma-10-things].

He played as a defender [s:lapresse-milan-profile]. He joined Barreirense in 1991 and made 91 appearances over four seasons [s:asroma-10-things]. He later moved to Porto, never played a match for them, and was loaned out several times [s:asroma-10-things]. His last club as a player was Estrela da Amadora, and he retired in 2005 [s:asroma-10-things] [s:lapresse-milan-profile].

## Lower leagues to the first trophy

He began coaching in 2005 with the youth team of Estrela da Amadora, then worked in the lower tiers of Portuguese football [s:acmilan-appointment] [s:lapresse-milan-profile]. At Desportivo Aves he took a third-division side to the Portuguese Cup quarter-finals in two consecutive seasons and won promotion to the second division [s:asroma-10-things].

In 2012 he took Paços de Ferreira to third place in the Portuguese top flight, which earned a Champions League play-off place [s:asroma-10-things] [s:acmilan-appointment]. He joined Porto in 2013 and won the Portuguese Super Cup, his first major trophy as a coach [s:asroma-10-things]. La Presse says Porto dismissed him in March, and he returned to Paços de Ferreira the following season and finished eighth [s:lapresse-milan-profile]. He later led Braga to the Portuguese Cup, beating Porto on penalties in the final [s:asroma-10-things].

## Shakhtar Donetsk, 2016 to 2019

Shakhtar's own account dates his first day in charge to 1 June 2016 [s:shakhtar-leaves]. In just over three years the club says he won three Ukrainian league titles, three Ukrainian Cups and one Ukrainian Super Cup, and three league and cup doubles [s:shakhtar-leaves]. In 139 games in charge his side won 103 [s:shakhtar-leaves] [s:acmilan-appointment]. The club also recorded an unbeaten run of 29 league games during his time there [s:shakhtar-leaves]. Shakhtar's own page calls him one of the most successful coaches in the club's history and says the side won every cup meeting under him [s:shakhtar-leaves]. Fonseca left in June 2019 [s:shakhtar-leaves].

AC Milan's announcement of his hiring in 2024 gives the same total, seven trophies and 103 wins in 139 matches at Shakhtar [s:acmilan-appointment].

## Roma, 2019 to 2021

AS Roma appointed him on 11 June 2019 [s:asroma-10-things]. In his first season the team finished fifth in Serie A, and the next season it reached the Europa League semi-finals [s:ligue1-article] [s:acmilan-appointment]. Roma dismissed him in May 2021 [s:lapresse-milan-profile].

## Lille, then Milan

Lille hired him for a two-season spell [s:dn-lyon-appointment]. The club finished fifth in Ligue 1 in 2022/23 and fourth in 2023/24 [s:dn-lyon-appointment]. In 2024 Lille reached the quarter-finals of the Europa Conference League [s:ligue1-article].

AC Milan announced his appointment on 13 June 2024 on a three-year contract [s:acmilan-appointment]. He was dismissed at the end of 2024 [s:dn-lyon-appointment]. Milan's announcement quoted no words from Fonseca himself, only the club's welcome to him and his staff [s:acmilan-appointment].

## Lyon from January 2025

Olympique Lyonnais appointed him on 31 January 2025 to replace Pierre Sage [s:ligue1-article] [s:lyon-appointment-release]. The club's filing says the contract runs until 30 June 2027 and that the decision was taken unanimously by John Textor and the sporting management [s:lyon-appointment-release]. The club described the hiring as part of a drive to restore its ambition [s:ligue1-article]. Lyon were sixth in Ligue 1 with 30 points at the time, and had already secured a place in the next Europa League phase [s:ligue1-article].
