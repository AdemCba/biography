## From Missouri to the draft

Anunoby was born on 17 July 1997 in London, United Kingdom [s:nba-bio] [s:br-anunoby]. He is listed at 6-7 (2.01 metres) and 240 pounds [s:nba-bio] [s:br-anunoby]. He played high school basketball at Jefferson City High School in Missouri, where he was named a Missouri Basketball Association Class 5 All-State player and a finalist for Mr. Basketball in Missouri [s:nba-bio].

He then played college basketball at Indiana [s:nba-bio] [s:br-anunoby]. In his last season there he played 16 games, limited by a knee injury that required surgery [s:idsnews-draft-2017]. He waited to hear his name at the June 2017 draft, and Toronto selected him 23rd overall in the first round [s:idsnews-draft-2017] [s:nba-bio].

## Toronto and the steals lead

Anunoby spent his early seasons with the Toronto Raptors [s:nba-trade-2023]. The NBA lists him as having helped Toronto win the 2019 championship [s:nba-bio]. His time in Toronto ended in December 2023, when the Raptors traded him to New York [s:nba-trade-2023].

In 2022-23 he led the whole league in steals, averaging 1.7 per game and finishing with 128 in total [s:nba-bio]. That season he was also named to the All-Defensive Second Team, alongside Miami Heat center Bam Adebayo [s:nba-all-defensive-2023].

## The December 2023 trade

On 30 December 2023 the Raptors traded Anunoby to New York [s:nba-trade-2023]. The Knicks sent RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley to Toronto, along with a 2024 second-round pick, and received Anunoby, Malachi Flynn and Precious Achiuwa in return [s:nba-trade-2023].

NBA.com wrote that the Knicks were giving up "two of their top offensive players" in Barrett and Quickley, and that the deal should provide a needed boost to their defense [s:nba-trade-2023]. The article pointed to Anunoby's steals lead from the season before as the reason [s:nba-trade-2023].
