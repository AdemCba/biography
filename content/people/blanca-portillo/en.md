Blanca Portillo has worked in three fields: the theatre, the cinema and television. She was born in Madrid on 15 June 1963 [s:ecartelera-portillo]. By eCartelera's count she has 17 films and 11 series to her name across 29 years [s:ecartelera-portillo]. She appeared in two films by Pedro Almodóvar, *Volver* and *Broken Embraces* [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo] [s:ecartelera-portillo].

## Amateur stages and the RESAD years

She made her stage debut at 17 with an amateur group [s:seminci-espiga-2023]. Stage came first. She then trained at RESAD and started professional stage work [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo]. Her first review, in the newspaper Diario 16, dates from the mid-1980s [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo].

In 1998 she won the Premio Unión de Actores for Best Supporting Actress in theatre for *Eslavos* [s:sensacine-portillo-biografia]. Her later stage work includes productions with the playwright Juan Mayorga and the director Tomaž Pandur, and a *Life Is a Dream* staged by Helena Pimenta [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo]. She also played Saint Teresa in a production directed by Paula Ortiz [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo].

## First films and early recognition

Her film debut was *Rojas*, directed by Azucena Rodríguez, in 1995 [s:sensacine-portillo]. Cinema came later. A year later she appeared in *El perro del hortelano* [s:ecartelera-portillo]. In 1998 she received a Goya nomination for Best New Actress for *El color de las nubes* [s:sensacine-portillo-biografia].

Her filmography in the following years includes *Los fantasmas de Goya*, where she played María Luisa de Parma, and *Elsa & Fred*, where she played Cuca [s:ecartelera-filmografia]. *Alatriste* (2006), *Siete mesas de billar francés* (2007) and *Los abrazos rotos* (2009) follow in the same list [s:ecartelera-portillo]. *La chispa de la vida* (2011) gave her the role of Mercedes San Román [s:ecartelera-filmografia] [s:ecartelera-portillo]. Later she appeared in *Invisibles* (2020) [s:ecartelera-portillo].

## Television: 7 Vidas and Hospital Central

Television made her a familiar face to Spanish audiences. SensaCine notes that the general public knows her best for her role in *7 Vidas* [s:sensacine-portillo]. In that Telecinco series she played Carlota Pérez, and she also appeared in *Acusados* as Rosa Ballester [s:ecartelera-filmografia].

Her credits include *7 Vidas* (1999 to 2006), *Hospital Central* (2000 to 2012) and *Cuéntame cómo pasó* (2001 to 2023) [s:ecartelera-portillo]. The Premio Unión de Actores she received in 2001 as Best TV Actress was for *7 Vidas* [s:sensacine-portillo].

Her TV credits also include the TV film *Concepción Arenal, la visitadora de cárceles*, whose role centres on Concepción Arenal's work to protect women prisoners. The source gives no broadcast year [s:ecartelera-filmografia].

## Almodóvar, Cannes and the Max Awards

The Seminci tribute describes *Volver* (2006) as her breakthrough, and it notes that she also co-starred in *Broken Embraces* [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo]. The Cannes Film Festival prize for Best Actress that year went to the whole female cast of *Volver* [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo].

She won two Max Awards. In 2002 she received the Best Actress award for *Madre, el drama padre*, and in 2004 the Best Supporting Actress award for *Como en las mejores familias* [s:sensacine-portillo].

## Seven Billiard Tables and the San Sebastián prize

In 2007 her performance in *Siete mesas de billar francés* earned the Best Actress prize at the San Sebastián International Film Festival [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo]. The Seminci tribute also records her Corral de Comedias Award for her career at the Almagro Festival [s:seminci-homenaje-portillo].

## Maixabel and the 2022 Goya

*Maixabel* (2021), directed by Icíar Bollaín, is the film that brought her the Goya [s:seminci-espiga-2023] [s:ecartelera-portillo]. She won the Goya for Best Lead Actress at the 2022 ceremony [s:espinof-goya-2022]. The Seminci press release describes her as "forged as an actress on the stage" [s:seminci-espiga-2023], and it credits her with four decades in the profession [s:seminci-espiga-2023].

## Honorary awards: Alicante and Seminci

In February 2018 the Alicante Film Festival announced that its Premio de Honor for the 15th edition would go to Blanca Portillo [s:officialpress-alicante-2018]. In 2023 she received the Espiga de Honor, the honorary award of the Seminci festival in Valladolid, at its 68th edition [s:seminci-espiga-2023]. The festival's press release, dated 23 October 2023, describes the honour as recognising a career marked by success both on stage and behind the screen [s:seminci-espiga-2023].
