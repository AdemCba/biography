Stan Wawrinka won three Grand Slam singles titles, and he is now ending his career in Basel. He won the Australian Open in 2014, Roland-Garros in 2015 and the US Open in 2016 [s:roland-garros-profile]. His career-high singles ranking was world No. 3 [s:atp-bio]. In January 2026 he announced that this would be his final season [s:roland-garros-profile]. Swiss Indoors Basel has been presented as his farewell tournament [s:swiss-indoors-farewell].

## Early years in Lausanne

Wawrinka was born on 28 March 1985 in Lausanne, Switzerland [s:roland-garros-profile]. Tennis.com describes him as a Vaud-born Swiss player [s:tennis-com-retirement]. As a junior he won the boys' singles title at Roland-Garros in 2003 [s:roland-garros-profile]. He later won the same tournament as a professional, on the same clay.

Official profiles do not agree on when his professional career began. The Australian Open profile gives 2002 as the year he turned professional [s:ausopen-profile]. The Roland-Garros profile gives 2004 in its text, a year after the junior title [s:roland-garros-profile]. This page does not choose between them.

## Three Grand Slam titles

In 2014 Wawrinka beat Rafael Nadal in the Australian Open final [s:roland-garros-profile]. A year later he won Roland-Garros, beating Novak Djokovic in the final [s:roland-garros-profile]. In 2016 he beat Djokovic again, this time in the US Open final [s:roland-garros-profile]. Three titles, three different tournaments.

Over his career the ATP profile lists 16 singles titles [s:atp-bio]. The Australian Open and Roland-Garros profiles give 16 singles and three doubles titles [s:ausopen-profile] [s:roland-garros-profile]. The Roland-Garros profile records a career singles record of 590 wins and 394 defeats, and career prize money of $38,742,890 [s:roland-garros-profile].

## Olympic and national honours

At the 2008 Beijing Games he won the men's doubles gold medal alongside Roger Federer [s:roland-garros-profile]. He was also part of the Swiss team that won the Davis Cup in 2014 [s:roland-garros-profile]. According to the ATP profile, he carried the Swiss flag at the 2012 London Olympics and was named Swiss of the Year in 2013 [s:atp-bio].

## Injuries, a return and Magnus Norman

Magnus Norman is listed as his coach on the Roland-Garros and Australian Open profiles [s:roland-garros-profile] [s:ausopen-profile]. In October 2022 in Basel, Wawrinka beat Casper Ruud in two sets. The report from that week said Norman had returned to his box after a long absence and would keep working with him, including at the Australian Open [s:tennisnet-2022].

That report also noted that he had suffered injuries and an operation over the previous two years [s:tennisnet-2022]. He said: "Coming back wasn't easy, I was struggling to get back in shape both physically and as a game" [s:tennisnet-2022]. He wanted to close his book with Norman. Ideally, that would be with a result like the Basel win [s:tennisnet-2022].

## Basel as a recurring stage

Wawrinka has described the crowd support at the 2022 Swiss Indoors as the best memory he has of the event [s:bluewin-2025]. By October 2025 he had played his 70th ATP tournament match in Switzerland, his 36th at a home tournament [s:bluewin-2025]. That report recorded his ranking at the time as 150th. He said he still believed he could beat top-50 players, while accepting that not every day would go that way [s:bluewin-2025].

## Playing on after 40

Ahead of the 2025 Basel event, he stressed how much recovery mattered at 40 [s:bluewin-2025]. He had opened that tournament with a two-set win over Miomir Kecmanovic and was due to face Casper Ruud or Quentin Halys in the round of 16 [s:bluewin-2025]. Tennis.com later reported that he reached the third round of the 2026 Australian Open and returned to the top 100 in February and March [s:tennis-com-retirement].

## The final season and the Basel farewell

In May 2026 the Swiss Indoors Basel opened ticket pre-sales for a farewell evening [s:swiss-indoors-farewell]. The tournament's announcement read: "Basel marks the end of the line for three-time Grand Slam winner Stan Wawrinka as he says goodbye to the game" [s:swiss-indoors-farewell]. It described the evening as "An emotional evening full of memories, surprises and magical moments" [s:swiss-indoors-farewell].

That farewell was set for Super Monday, 26 October 2026, at 6 p.m. Tennis.com also reported that he would be honoured at Roland-Garros, where he won the title in 2015 [s:tennis-com-retirement]. The 2026 Swiss Indoors runs from 24 October to 1 November as an ATP 500 event, its 55th edition [s:swiss-indoors-farewell].

## A last tournament before the end

On 22 October 2026 he lost in the second round at Lyon to top seed Flavio Cobolli, 6-2, 6-4 [s:tennisuptodate-lyon]. That was his penultimate tournament. Basel is the last one [s:tennisuptodate-lyon]. A farewell exhibition in Geneva on 19 December has also been reported [s:tennisuptodate-lyon].

## October 2026

Stan Wawrinka received a standing ovation at the Swiss Indoors in Basel on Monday, 26 October 2026 [s:atp-video] [s:tenniswatcher]. The ATP had announced a farewell ceremony at St. Jakobshalle for that Monday [s:atp-news-basel-fc]. TennisWatcher describes an emotional ceremony that lasted more than an hour, with the ovation on Centre Court [s:tenniswatcher].

Roger Brennwald called him "the Sylvester Stallone of tennis" and presented him with a golden ball. A giant portrait of Wawrinka was spray-painted live on court. The Swiss singer Bastian Baker, a close friend, performed a song for him, and video messages were shown on the big screen, including one from Roger Federer [s:tenniswatcher].

Wawrinka said: "I always gave the best of myself, but I never dreamed of winning Grand Slams" [s:tenniswatcher].

On Tuesday, 27 October, he played eighth seed Jiri Lehecka in the first round [s:atp-news-basel-fc] [s:tennis365-farewell]. He lost 5-7, 6-7(2). The match ended at 23:02 local time, and he lost the tiebreak 2-7 [s:srf-lehecka].

He retired after the match [s:tennis365-farewell]. About the farewell he said: "For me, it's an ideal end, it's a dream end" [s:tennis365-farewell]. The ATP had described Basel as his 19th and final appearance at the Swiss Indoors [s:atp-news-basel-fc].
