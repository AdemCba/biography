Before he won anything, Roger Federer fetched balls. In 1993 and 1994 he worked as a ballboy at the tournament in his home town of Basel [s:atp-bio]. Three decades later he left the sport with 20 Grand Slam titles.

## Basel beginnings

Federer was born on 8 August 1981 in Basel, Switzerland [s:britannica]. His father Robert is Swiss and his mother Lynette is South African; he has a sister, Diana [s:atp-bio]. He started playing at eight and grew up watching Stefan Edberg, Boris Becker and Pete Sampras [s:atp-bio]. Britannica dates his professional career from 1998 [s:britannica].

## Twenty majors

The headline number is 20 Grand Slam singles titles: six at the Australian Open, one at the French Open, eight at Wimbledon and five at the US Open [s:britannica]. Eight Wimbledon titles is a men's record [s:britannica], and in 2018 he became the first man to reach 20 majors [s:britannica].

The hardest one to get was Paris. Federer's first 13 major titles all came elsewhere. In June 2009 he finally won the French Open, beating Robin Soderling 6-1, 7-6, 6-4 in the final [s:cnn-2009]. The win tied Pete Sampras's record of 14 majors at the time and made him one of only six men to have won all four Grand Slam tournaments [s:cnn-2009]. Asked how it felt, he said he could now play "with my mind at peace" [s:cnn-2009].

## Numbers that last

Grand Slams are only part of the record. Federer won 103 titles and 1,251 matches; in the Open Era only Jimmy Connors won more matches [s:atp-bio]. He held the No. 1 ranking for 237 consecutive weeks, a record in the history of the ATP Rankings [s:atp-bio]. At the Olympics he won doubles gold in 2008 and singles silver in 2012 [s:britannica].

## Family and foundation

Federer is married to Mirka Vavrinec, a former professional who reached a career-high No. 76 in the WTA rankings. They have two sets of twins: daughters Myla and Charlene, and sons Leo and Lenny [s:atp-bio].

In 2003 he set up the Roger Federer Foundation, which funds education projects in Southern Africa and Switzerland. According to the ATP, it has reached more than a million children and invested over $50 million [s:atp-bio].

## Retirement

His knee decided it [s:nbc-laver]. On 15 September 2022, aged 41, Federer announced that after three years of injuries and surgeries his body had sent him a clear message, and that the Laver Cup in London the following week would be his last ATP event [s:retirement-statement]. "Tennis has treated me more generously than I ever would have dreamt," he wrote [s:retirement-statement].

He played one match there, a doubles on 23 September. His partner was Rafael Nadal, the rival he had called to ask for "one last doubles" together. They lost to Jack Sock and Frances Tiafoe 4-6, 7-6, 11-9 [s:nbc-laver].
