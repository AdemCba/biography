Troy Aikman is in the news because of what comes after this season. On October 15, 2026, he told The Ticket in Dallas that he would finish the season with ESPN and then become a free agent [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026]. For most fans, though, Aikman is still the Dallas quarterback who was taken first overall in 1989 and went on to win three Super Bowls [s:pfhof-profile].

## The contract question

Aikman's own words are the clearest guide to where things stand. “I'll broadcast through this season, and I'll be a free agent at the end of the year and kind of see what happens,” he said [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026]. Asked about the reported tension with Buck, he did not discuss it directly. He said there was “probably a time and a place for all that to be discussed,” but added, “I don't know if now is the time” [s:newsweek-aikman-buck].

According to The Athletic, Buck agreed to a six-year deal in September. Front Office Sports reported that Aikman had not received an offer from ESPN [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026]. The Sun-Times, which summarised that reporting, also noted that Buck addressed the tension on air before a Bills and Rams broadcast, which defused the situation [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026].

ESPN will air its first Super Bowl at the end of this season, and a rift between Aikman and Buck could overshadow that run-up [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026]. ## Oklahoma, then UCLA

Aikman was born on November 21, 1966, in West Covina, California [s:cfbhall-aikman] [s:harry-walker-aikman]. He was raised in Cerritos, California, and his family later moved to Henryetta, Oklahoma, when he was 12 [s:harry-walker-aikman] [s:espn-press-bio]. That move matters for his college story, because he first played for the University of Oklahoma, from 1984 to 1985 [s:cfbhall-aikman].

He then transferred to UCLA, where the College Football Hall of Fame says he made an “immediate and indelible impact” in two seasons [s:cfbhall-aikman]. He was a consensus first-team All-American, finished third in Heisman Trophy voting, and won the Davey O'Brien Award [s:cfbhall-aikman]. After his junior season he was named Pac-10 Offensive Player of the Year [s:cfbhall-aikman]. His UCLA numbers included 5,436 career passing yards, and single-season records for completions (228) and completion percentage (64.8) [s:cfbhall-aikman]. The Bruins won the 1987 Aloha Bowl and the 1989 Cotton Bowl with him at quarterback [s:cfbhall-aikman]. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 2008 [s:cfbhall-aikman].

## The first overall pick

Dallas used the first overall selection in the 1989 NFL Draft on Aikman, and he spent his entire playing career with the Cowboys [s:pfhof-profile]. The Pro Football Hall of Fame notes that Dallas had finished 3-13 in 1988 [s:pfhof-1989-draft]. The Hall of Fame also says he is one of only 14 players in NFL draft history to go from first overall pick to Hall of Fame election [s:pfhof-1989-draft].

Aikman played his Cowboys career from 1989 through 2000 [s:tshof-aikman]. ESPN's biography says his 12 years in the league were highlighted by victories in Super Bowls XXVII, XXVIII and XXX [s:espn-press-bio].

## Three Super Bowls in Dallas

Aikman was the MVP of Super Bowl XXVII, when Dallas beat the Buffalo Bills 52-17 and he completed 22 of 30 passes [s:tshof-aikman]. Dallas went on to win Super Bowls XXVIII and XXX as well [s:pfhof-profile]. He is one of four quarterbacks to lead a team to victory in three Super Bowls [s:foxsports-bio].

The numbers across his career are just as clear. Aikman played in six Pro Bowls, from 1991 to 1996 [s:tshof-aikman]. His career totals include 32,942 passing yards and 165 touchdowns, and he is the Cowboys' all-time leader in passing yards, touchdowns, completions and attempts [s:tshof-aikman]. He was voted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in February 2006, in Canton, Ohio [s:espn-press-bio] [s:pfhof-profile].

## From Fox to Monday Night Football

Aikman's broadcasting career started at Fox. In 2001 he worked alongside Dick Stockton and former teammate Daryl Johnston on the network's No. 2 team [s:foxsports-bio]. After one season in the booth he was moved up to Fox's lead NFL team, alongside Joe Buck and Cris Collinsworth [s:foxsports-bio]. Harry Walker, his speaking agency, describes him as Fox's lead NFL game analyst for more than two decades [s:harry-walker-aikman].

He then moved to ESPN, where he became the network's new NFL game analyst on Monday Night Football, in the booth with Buck [s:espn-press-bio]. ESPN's press materials describe the pair as the NFL's most consistent broadcasting tandem [s:espn-press-bio]. The broadcast won a Sports Emmy for Outstanding Live Sports Series [s:espn-press-bio]. Buck is himself an Emmy-winning play-by-play announcer [s:foxsports-bio].

The pair's longevity is the reason the current talks matter. The Sun-Times reports that Buck and Aikman have worked together for 25 years, and that in 2023, their second season at ESPN, they passed Pat Summerall and John Madden as the longest-tenured NFL broadcast pair [s:suntimes-aikman-buck-2026]. An older Fox biography described the duo as second only to Summerall and Madden, which is consistent with that timeline [s:foxsports-bio].
