Hugh Michael Jackman was born on 12 October 1968 in Sydney, Australia [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Biography.com credits his early stage work with critical praise, including his Curly in Oklahoma! in London [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. The milestones below come from award bodies, Playbill, Variety and Biography.com.

## Wolverine and the X-Men films

He first earned wide attention by playing the mutant Wolverine in X-Men (2000), a role Biography.com says was first portrayed in the comic books and then on screen [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Swordfish and Kate and Leopold followed in 2001 [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. The Golden Globes list Kate & Leopold among his nominations, at the 2002 ceremony, in the Best Performance by an Actor in a Motion Picture, Musical or Comedy category [s:golden-globes-jackman].

He returned as Wolverine in X2 (2003) and X-Men: The Last Stand (2006). The spinoff X-Men Origins: Wolverine followed in 2009 [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Box Office Mojo lists 20th Century Fox as its distributor, with a domestic gross of $179,883,157 and a worldwide gross of $373,062,864 [s:box-office-mojo-x-men-origins]. His last appearance as the character came in Logan, the 2017 hit [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

Outside the X-Men series, he faced Christian Bale in the historical drama The Prestige (2006). The Fountain, the same year, gave him three different characters to play [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Biography.com also records that he was offered the role of James Bond in Casino Royale (2006) and turned it down [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

## Broadway, the Tony and the Emmy

Jackman won the Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical in 2004 for playing Peter Allen in The Boy from Oz [s:playbill-2004-tony-winners]. Playbill's report, dated 7 June 2004, describes him as the Tony host who also took the award [s:playbill-2004-tony-winners]. Biography.com adds that he also claimed an Emmy for his work as host of the 2004 Tony Awards [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

He returned to the New York stage in 2009 in A Steady Rain [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Two years later he brought his song-and-dance act to Hugh Jackman: Back on Broadway [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Biography.com notes that he used a similar format for The Man. The Music. The Show. in 2019 [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

## Les Misérables, the Oscar nomination and the Golden Globe

Jackman received a Golden Globe for Best Performance by an Actor in a Motion Picture, Musical or Comedy, for Les Misérables, in January 2013 [s:golden-globes-jackman] [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. The Golden Globes list four nominations and one win for him in total [s:golden-globes-jackman]. Les Misérables was distributed by Universal Pictures, and Box Office Mojo records a domestic gross of $149,260,140 and a worldwide gross of $442,757,529 [s:box-office-mojo-les-miserables].

The same role brought his first Academy Award nomination [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. He was nominated for Best Actor in a Leading Role at the 85th Academy Awards, and Daniel Day-Lewis won that category for Lincoln [s:oscars-2013-ceremony].

The Hollywood Walk of Fame gave him a star on 13 December 2012 [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

## Later film and television work

Van Helsing (2004) and the mystery Scoop (2006) were among his next films [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Viva Laughlin, his 2007 CBS series, lasted just two episodes before it was canceled [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Real Steel (2011) cast him as a father who rebuilds his relationship with his son [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. His 2008 film Deception earned only $4.6 million at the box office [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

Pan (2015) cast him as the villain [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Movie 43 (2013) gave him a lighter role after Les Misérables [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. Eddie the Eagle (2016) saw him as ski coach Bronson Peary [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

The Greatest Showman (2017) is the film in which he played P.T. Barnum, and it earned him a Golden Globe nomination at the 2018 ceremony, in the Musical or Comedy category [s:biography-hugh-jackman] [s:golden-globes-jackman]. He appeared in the animated feature Missing Link (2019) [s:biography-hugh-jackman], and that year he teamed with Allison Janney in the true-story film Bad Education [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

He also hosted the Academy Awards in 2009 [s:biography-hugh-jackman]. People magazine named him its Sexiest Man Alive in December 2008 [s:biography-hugh-jackman].

The Greatest Showman grossed $174,340,174 domestically and $472,132,453 worldwide [s:box-office-mojo-greatest-showman].

## Treasure Island

Ridley Scott will direct a new Treasure Island from a script by Jack Thorne, the Emmy-winning writer of Adolescence [s:variety-au-treasure-island-cast]. Variety calls it a new adaptation of Robert Louis Stevenson's 1883 novel [s:variety-au-treasure-island-cast]. Variety described the role as "another classic character" added to his filmography, the pirate Long John Silver [s:variety-au-treasure-island-cast].

Variety's June 2026 report named Scott Free, Scott's production banner, with Michael Pruss producing alongside Scott, and it named no distributor [s:variety-au-treasure-island-cast].

By October, Variety reported that Sony had slated the film. That report, dated 1 October 2026, covers the 2027 release dates for both 24 Jump Street and Treasure Island [s:variety-au-treasure-island-release]. Treasure Island hits theatres first on 11 November 2027, and Thorne will executive produce [s:variety-au-treasure-island-release]. The film adapts Robert Louis Stevenson's novel [s:variety-au-treasure-island-release].
