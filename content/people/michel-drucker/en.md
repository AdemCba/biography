## Sport first, then the ORTF

Michel Drucker was born on 12 September 1942 in Vire, in the Calvados department of Normandy, about 60 kilometres south of Caen [s:connexion-france-2024]. In a July 2026 interview he said he was about to start his 64th season on television in September, and that he would be 84 [s:parismatch-2026-07-26]. Sport was his first profession, and he still follows it closely [s:parismatch-2026-07-26].

His career officially began in 1965 in the sports department of the ORTF, the French state broadcaster of the time [s:ozap-2011-champs-elysees]. INA's archive dates his television debut to 1965 as well [s:ina-mediaclip-michel-drucker].

Connexion France says he moved to Paris in the early 1960s and later landed a sports journalism job on the programme Sports Dimanche, without giving a date [s:connexion-france-2024]. In a June 2022 interview he described himself as a native of Basse-Normandie who was welcomed in Provence, and said he had always felt more provincial than Parisian [s:dhnet-2022-france3].

## From variety shows to Champs-Elysees

INA's archive lists Tilt, a variety magazine he presented, with a broadcast date of 5 October 1966 [s:ina-mediaclip-michel-drucker]. The same archive credits him with Les Rendez-vous du dimanche, which he hosted for eight years [s:ina-mediaclip-michel-drucker].

Champs-Elysees began in January 1982. Ozap says it was created on 9 January 1982 at the Espace Cardin and moved to the Studio Gabriel a year later [s:ozap-2011-champs-elysees]. INA gives 16 January 1982 as the broadcast date of the first issue [s:ina-mediaclip-michel-drucker]. The two dates are a week apart, so this page gives only the month.

Connexion France says he hosted the show from 1982 to 1990 and returned to it from 2010 to 2013 [s:connexion-france-2024]. Ozap says the first run lasted eight years and that he left public service in 1990 [s:ozap-2011-champs-elysees]. On Antenne 2, Ozap says he was given the early-evening Saturday prime-time slots [s:ozap-2011-champs-elysees].

## Vivement Dimanche, from France 2 to France 3

Since 1998 he has held the Sunday afternoon slot with Vivement Dimanche [s:ozap-2011-champs-elysees]. The programme aired on France 2 from 1998 to 2022 and moved to France 3 in August 2022 [s:connexion-france-2024]. By then he had spent 36 years on France 2 [s:dhnet-2022-france3].

When the move was announced in June 2022, he said he was moving to a channel that suits his personality [s:dhnet-2022-france3].

By July 2026 he had presented the programme for almost 25 years, and he said it keeps renewing itself [s:parismatch-2026-07-26]. His last Vivement Dimanche episode aired on 28 June [s:parismatch-2026-07-26].

He also said the show would move closer to the regions after the change of channel, a point the DH Net article paraphrases rather than quotes [s:dhnet-2022-france3].

## Three Sundays off air in 2026

Vivement Dimanche is not always on air on France 3 on a Sunday. Tendance Ouest reported that the programme was not shown on 3 May 2026, when France 3 broadcast a rugby match, a Challenge Cup semi-final between Montpellier and the Dragons [s:tendanceouest-2026-05-03]. The article says the channel had made similar changes for major cycling races in earlier weeks, naming Paris-Roubaix and Liège-Bastogne-Liège, and that no return date had been confirmed [s:tendanceouest-2026-05-03].

On 18 October 2026, Stars-Actu reported that France 3 changed its usual schedule to make room for rugby. It did not name the match [s:stars-actu-2026-10-18].

For Sunday 1 November 2026, the Toussaint weekend, Titres Presse reports that France 3 pulled Michel Drucker and Vivement Dimanche from the schedule, citing Toutelatele [s:titrespresse-drucker-1-nov]. The article says the channel changed its strategy for that weekend but gives no explicit reason [s:titrespresse-drucker-1-nov]. The sources reviewed for this page do not give a reason for that Sunday, and the article does not say when the programme returns.

The reports above are press coverage, not a broadcaster's schedule. Anyone checking a specific Sunday should compare them with the France 3 listings before relying on them. The two reports that name a reason both point to rugby or cycling on France 3, although Stars-Actu does not name the rugby match [s:tendanceouest-2026-05-03] [s:stars-actu-2026-10-18].
