## Why the comparison is getting attention

Eli Lilly's experimental drug retatrutide was tested directly against Ozempic, and Lilly announced the topline results on 28 October 2026 [s:fierce-retatrutide-ozempic] [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Lilly's July readouts compared the drug with placebo, while this trial compares it with semaglutide 2 mg, the Ozempic dose [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3] [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. These are topline results, and the full data have not yet been published [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide].

## What the trial showed

The trial is registered as TRANSCEND-T2D-2 (NCT06260722). The registry describes it as a randomized, open-label phase 3 study sponsored by Eli Lilly [s:clinicaltrials-nct06260722]. It enrolled 1,298 adults with type 2 diabetes whose blood sugar was not controlled well enough on metformin, with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. The registry lists an estimated enrollment of 1,250 [s:clinicaltrials-nct06260722], while the reported results describe 1,298 participants randomized [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Participants were assigned in a 2:2:1 ratio to retatrutide 9 mg, retatrutide 12 mg or semaglutide 2 mg, the Ozempic dose, and treatment ran for 80 weeks [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide].

Blood sugar was tracked as HbA1c. The mean at the start was 8.3% [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Both retatrutide doses lowered it by 2.2 percentage points at 80 weeks, against 1.8 points for semaglutide [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide] [s:fierce-retatrutide-ozempic]. The 12 mg dose was superior to semaglutide (P < .001), and the 9 mg dose was noninferior [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. The 9 mg superiority result is nominal and falls outside the trial's error control for multiple comparisons [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide], and Lilly described the 9 mg gap against Ozempic as nominal [s:biopharmadive-retatrutide-ozempic].

Weight loss was larger on both retatrutide doses [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Participants on 9 mg lost an average of 19.5% of body weight and those on 12 mg lost 20.9%, against 9.4% on semaglutide, with both differences significant at P < .001 [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide] [s:fierce-retatrutide-ozempic]. These efficacy figures assume people stayed on treatment for the full 80 weeks without starting another glucose-lowering drug [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide].

The trade-off was tolerability [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Treatment was stopped because of adverse events in 7.9% of people on 12 mg and 4.8% on 9 mg, against 2.7% on semaglutide [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Dysesthesia was reported in 6.5% on 12 mg and 4.8% on 9 mg, compared with 2.7% on semaglutide [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide]. Diarrhea affected up to 32.7% on retatrutide and 26.4% on Ozempic [s:fierce-retatrutide-ozempic].

## Background on the drug and its earlier trials

Lilly describes retatrutide as a triple agonist that targets the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3]. In July 2026 Lilly announced results from two other phase 3 trials, TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3, which it described as successful [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3]. TRIUMPH-2 enrolled 1,152 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes, and at 80 weeks the 12 mg dose produced a 20.8% weight loss against 4.0% on placebo [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3]. TRIUMPH-3 enrolled 1,949 adults with severe obesity and established cardiovascular disease, and its 12 mg dose produced a 22.6% weight loss against 3.2% on placebo [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3]. Both of those trials compared retatrutide with placebo rather than with an approved drug [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3].

## What comes next

Retatrutide has not been approved. Lilly plans to submit a Biologics License Application to the FDA in the first quarter of 2027 [s:biospace-lilly-triumph-2-3] [s:fierce-retatrutide-ozempic]. The 28 October announcement was topline, and detailed data are to be presented at a future meeting and published in a peer-reviewed journal, with no dates given [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide].

The registry lists the study as active, not recruiting, with a primary completion date of 27 August 2026 [s:clinicaltrials-nct06260722]. Lilly reported greater improvements than semaglutide in non-HDL cholesterol, triglycerides and systolic blood pressure, but did not report the size of those differences [s:ajmc-retatrutide-semaglutide].
