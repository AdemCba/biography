"Winter storm warning" is trending in the United States, and one of the alerts in the news is a warning from the National Weather Service in Anchorage for Alaska's Northern Susitna Valley. The forecast calls for 7 to 15 inches of snow, with the warning running through Friday, October 30 [s:yahoo-nws-15] [s:ssbcrack-alaska].

## What the warning covers

The warning covers the Northern Susitna Valley, including the Parks Highway corridor through Broad Pass, the Denali Highway and Chulitna [s:yahoo-nws-15] [s:ssbcrack-alaska]. The Parks Highway is the main road between Anchorage and Fairbanks, so the warning reaches one of the state's key road links [s:ssbcrack-alaska].

Total accumulation is forecast at 7 to 15 inches [s:yahoo-nws-15]. The articles do not agree on how much snow falls before the warning starts. One puts it at 3 to 6 inches [s:yahoo-nws-15], and another at 2 to 4 inches [s:yahoo-announced].

Forecasters expect rapidly accumulating snow and visibility of one-quarter mile or less at times [s:yahoo-nws-15] [s:ssbcrack-alaska]. The article explains what the alert means: a winter storm warning for snow means severe winter weather conditions will make travel very hazardous [s:yahoo-announced].

## When the snow falls

The warning takes effect at 1 p.m. Thursday and ends at 2 p.m. Friday, Alaska time [s:yahoo-announced]. In Eastern Time, the same window runs from 5 p.m. Thursday to 6 p.m. Friday [s:yahoo-nws-15].

The heaviest snow is expected Thursday night through Friday morning [s:ssbcrack-alaska] [s:yahoo-announced]. Temperatures are forecast to rise above freezing (32°F) on Friday afternoon, so the snow could mix with or change to rain and leave a wet slush layer on top of the new snow [s:ssbcrack-alaska].

Travel could be very difficult along the Parks Highway, where low visibility and quickly piling snow are the main concerns [s:yahoo-announced].

## Why the timing matters

The window is about 25 hours long, from 1 p.m. Thursday to 2 p.m. Friday Alaska time [s:yahoo-announced]. Friday is the last day of the warning, and the switch from snow to rain is the detail most likely to change road conditions late in the event [s:ssbcrack-alaska]. Drivers planning trips through the Susitna Valley on Thursday or Friday face the heaviest snow during the same hours [s:ssbcrack-alaska] [s:yahoo-announced].

The two reports give slightly different snow totals before the warning begins, so the full storm total depends on which early estimate proves closer [s:yahoo-nws-15] [s:yahoo-announced].

## What to watch next

Watch for updates from the National Weather Service in Anchorage on whether the warning is extended, shortened or cancelled before 2 p.m. Alaska time on Friday [s:yahoo-announced]. The rain-or-snow forecast for Friday afternoon is the next detail to confirm, because it decides whether the storm ends as snow or as slush [s:ssbcrack-alaska].

Snow totals may change as the storm arrives. The current forecast range is 7 to 15 inches, and the final figure will show whether the storm reached the upper end of that range [s:yahoo-nws-15].
