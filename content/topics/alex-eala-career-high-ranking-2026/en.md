Alex Eala has a new best ranking and one more tournament to push it. The WTA list released on 2 November 2026 has her at No. 17, up two places from No. 19 and past her previous high of No. 18 [s:gma-no17]. The WTA profile now shows the same number for her current and career-high ranking [s:wta-eala-profile].

## Where she stands now

The jump came from a quarterfinal at the Pan Pacific Open in Tokyo. Eala beat British wildcard Mika Stojsavljevic 6-1, 6-2 in the first round and Talia Gibson 4-6, 6-2, 6-0 in the second, then lost to Diana Shnaider in the last eight [s:tennis365-pan-pacific] [s:gma-no17]. Gibson was ranked No. 38 in the world, and Shnaider, her next opponent, was No. 13; Shnaider had also beaten Eala 7-5, 6-4 in the second round at Ningbo the week before [s:tennis365-pan-pacific]. The Gibson match was close for a set. She was broken at 3-4 and 4-5 then won the last two sets 6-2 and 6-0 [s:tennis365-pan-pacific].

Before Tokyo, the autumn had been flat. She exited early in Singapore, Wuhan and Ningbo [s:gma-no17]. In Singapore she lost in the round of 16 as the No. 3 seed, and her ranking slipped to No. 19 with 2,241 points [s:philstar-no19]. Part of that dip was a choice. She skipped the Beijing WTA 1000 to play the Asian Games for the Philippines, where she won bronze as the top seed [s:philstar-no19].

On the Tokyo live rankings, her 2,289 points left her 26 behind Naomi Osaka in 17th and 74 behind Amanda Anisimova in 16th [s:tennis365-pan-pacific]. Those are the players directly above her.

## The last event of her season

Eala's final WTA event of the year is the Hong Kong Open, a WTA 250, where she opens against Alycia Parks in the round of 32 [s:gma-no17]. The two have split their meetings so far. Parks beat her at the Australian Open, and Eala won the later match at the Canadian Open [s:gma-no17].

A year-end finish at No. 17 or better is the realistic target, and the coverage of her rise does not put a points figure on what Hong Kong is worth. What the numbers do show is how tight the table is. At the start of the Tokyo week she was 19th, Mboko, out since June with a knee injury, was falling from 15th to 20th, and the gap from Eala to Cirstea in 15th was 97 points [s:tennis365-pan-pacific]. A deep run in Hong Kong could move her again, and an early loss could let players behind her close in.

She will not be at the WTA Finals, which run from 8 to 15 November for the top eight players. Philstar reported that she had fallen to 15th in the season-long race and missed the cutoff [s:philstar-no19].

## How she got here

Eala was born on 23 May 2005 and is from Quezon City in the Philippines. She started playing at four, is left-handed, trains at the Rafael Nadal Academy in Mallorca and is coached by Joan Bosch [s:wta-eala-profile].

The breakthrough came in 2025, when she reached the Miami semifinals as a wildcard. She was the first Filipino to reach a WTA 1000 semifinal[s:wta-eala-profile]. This season she won the Birmingham WTA 125 and the Washington DC Open, her first WTA title, and reached the Wimbledon round of 16 and the Berlin semifinals [s:gma-no17] [s:wta-eala-profile].

Her climb through the year has been steady. She had been at a career-best No. 18 since August [s:philstar-no19]. A Gulf News report on that rise listed her wins over Iga Swiatek at Wimbledon and Jessica Pegula in Washington, and put her record against top-10 players at 9-4 [s:gulfnews-no18].

## What comes next

After Hong Kong, her season is over, and the next test is January. Her 2026 Australian Open ended in the round of 128, according to the season recap, and Parks was the opponent who beat her there [s:gma-no17]. A better ranking at the end of the year gives her a stronger position going into the 2027 draw. Seeding cutoffs for that event have not been published in the coverage cited here, so no exact number can be given.

For now the picture is simple. She is 21, she is at a career-high ranking, and she has one match against a familiar opponent to decide how the year ends [s:gma-no17] [s:philstar-no19].
