Nintendo's remake of *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* arrives on 5 November 2026 [s:thestar-aonuma-nervous-2026], and the platform is Nintendo Switch 2 only [s:gematsu-ocarina-remake-2026]. The 1998 Nintendo 64 original is the benchmark everyone will use.

## Why the launch is in the news

The trigger was an interview with Eiji Aonuma, the Nintendo producer who has steered the series for more than 25 years. Speaking to AFP in Paris, he said: "I'm all nervous about releasing the remake of 'Ocarina' because I don't know how it's going to turn out." He added that he "can't stop thinking about it" [s:thestar-aonuma-nervous-2026].

Other coverage put the worry down to fan expectations. LevelUp reports that Aonuma is concerned about how players will receive the game, given the place the original holds in their memories, and describes expectations as considerable [s:levelup-aonuma-nervous-2026]. Neither article says exactly what Aonuma fears will go wrong.

## What Nintendo announced in June

Nintendo revealed the remake in June 2026. Gematsu's report, dated 9 June, quotes the company's line: "The Nintendo 64 classic returns for a new generation in 2026, reborn exclusively for Nintendo Switch 2" [s:gematsu-ocarina-remake-2026].

The reveal came with a teaser trailer and a teaser website, while Nintendo said more information would follow later [s:gematsu-ocarina-remake-2026]. That left fans with a release window of 2026 for several months, which is why the 5 November date has drawn so much attention.

## The background of the project

The 1998 game is one of the most remembered titles in the series, and the remake is being treated as a major event in the saga's history. AFP calls it the most ambitious of the series' remakes and says its release is part of the saga's 40th anniversary [s:thestar-aonuma-nervous-2026].

The team also included people who never played the original. Aonuma credited them for the work: "It's thanks to these young people, by thinking about how to adapt the game in the best possible way" [s:thestar-aonuma-nervous-2026]. That detail matters for players who will compare the new version with memories of the 1998 game, because the people rebuilding it did not grow up with the same version.

## What to watch next

The first question is the release itself on 5 November, when the game reaches Switch 2 owners. The second is the reception. Aonuma's comments show that Nintendo knows the comparison with the original will dominate reviews and social media.

Because none of the reports lists the specific changes, the most useful details will come from the launch coverage and early hands-on impressions. Watch for what the remake changes in controls, visuals and dungeon design, and whether Nintendo's own comments about fan expectations match what players find.
